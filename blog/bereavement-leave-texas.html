Bereavement Leave in Texas: Does Your Employer Have to Give You Time Off After a Death?

Short answer

No law requires private employers in Texas to give bereavement or funeral leave, paid or unpaid. The U.S. Department of Labor says the FLSA doesn't require pay for time not worked, including attending a funeral, and the FMLA doesn't list bereavement as a qualifying reason. But you may still be protected: FMLA can cover leave to care for a seriously ill family member before a death, your own serious health condition such as severe depression afterward, and certain funeral-related needs when a service member dies on active duty. The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act can require time off to recover from a miscarriage. A religious mourning practice may need to be accommodated, and if your employer has a written bereavement policy, it should apply it consistently.

Losing someone is hard enough without worrying about your job. Many Houston employees are surprised to learn that bereavement leave is a company benefit, not a legal right in Texas. But several laws can still protect you during and after a loss, and an employer that treats grieving employees unequally can cross a legal line.

👉 Bereavement leave comes from your employer's policy. Other leave laws may fill the gaps.

1Is Bereavement Leave Required?

No federal law and no Texas law requires private employers to provide bereavement leave. The Department of Labor treats funeral leave as a matter of agreement between employer and employee. Many employers offer a few days by policy, and the policy decides who counts as family, whether it's paid and what proof is required.

Check your handbook

If a written policy promises paid bereavement leave, ask HR to apply it in writing. Applying it to some employees and not others can be evidence of discrimination.

2Laws That Can Protect You During a Loss

FMLA: before a deathUp to 12 weeks of job-protected leave to care for a spouse, child or parent with a serious health condition.
FMLA: your own healthGrief can lead to depression or other serious health conditions that qualify for FMLA leave.
Military familiesFMLA qualifying exigency leave can cover funeral arrangements when a service member dies on covered active duty.
Pregnancy lossThe Pregnant Workers Fairness Act can require time off to recover from a miscarriage or stillbirth.
Religious mourningEmployers with 15+ employees must reasonably accommodate religious mourning practices unless it causes undue hardship.
DisabilityA mental health condition after a loss can qualify for ADA accommodations such as a schedule change or short leave.

3When Being Fired After a Loss Is Illegal

Because Texas is at-will, an employer can generally discipline you for missing work without approved leave. But a firing may be illegal if:

  • You were on, or had requested, FMLA-protected leave
  • You needed time off for a miscarriage or pregnancy-related condition
  • You were fired for your depression or anxiety instead of being accommodated
  • Coworkers of a different race, sex, religion or age got bereavement leave you were denied

✅How to Request Time Off After a Death

  1. Read the bereavement policy and who it covers.
  2. Request leave in writing, even by text or email, and keep a copy.
  3. Ask about PTO, unpaid leave or FMLA if bereavement days aren't enough.
  4. If your health is affected, see a doctor and ask HR about FMLA or ADA accommodations.
  5. If you're disciplined or fired, write down how others were treated and talk to a lawyer.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is bereavement leave required by law in Texas?

No. Neither federal law nor Texas law requires private employers to provide bereavement or funeral leave. It is a benefit set by employer policy or agreement.

Does FMLA cover bereavement leave?

Generally no. Bereavement is not an FMLA qualifying reason, but FMLA can cover caring for a seriously ill family member before a death, your own serious health condition, and certain funeral needs when a service member dies on covered active duty.

Can I be fired for missing work for a funeral in Texas?

Possibly, because Texas is an at-will state. A firing may be illegal if it involved protected FMLA leave, a pregnancy-related condition, a disability, religious practice, or unequal treatment based on a protected characteristic.

Do I get time off after a miscarriage?

The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act can require employers with 15 or more employees to provide reasonable accommodations, including time off to recover from a miscarriage, unless it causes undue hardship.

Is bereavement leave paid in Texas?

Only if your employer's policy provides it. Federal law does not require pay for time not worked, including attending a funeral.
