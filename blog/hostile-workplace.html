What Is a Hostile Work Environment in Texas?

Short answer

A hostile work environment is illegal harassment based on a protected characteristic, such as sex, race, color, religion, national origin, age (40+), disability or pregnancy, that is severe or pervasive enough that a reasonable person would find the workplace intimidating, hostile or abusive. A mean boss, rudeness or a toxic culture alone usually isn't illegal. A single incident can be enough if it's serious, like a physical assault. In Texas, report the conduct to your employer, document it, and file a charge with the EEOC (generally 300 days) or the Texas Workforce Commission (180 days, or 300 days for sexual harassment). Retaliating against you for complaining is illegal.

"My workplace is hostile" is one of the most common things Houston employees tell a lawyer. Sometimes it's a legal claim, and sometimes it's a bad job. The difference comes down to why you're being targeted and how bad the conduct is.

Bullying that isn't tied to a protected characteristic generally isn't covered by federal or Texas harassment law, but it may still violate company policy. And if the "toxic" treatment started after you complained about discrimination, pay or safety, it may be retaliation.

2The Laws That Apply in Texas

  • Title VII and Texas Labor Code Chapter 21: harassment based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy), national origin; generally employers with 15+ employees.
  • Sexual harassment under Texas law: since 2021, Chapter 21 covers employers with one or more employees and people acting directly in the employer's interests, and the employer is liable if it knew or should have known and didn't take immediate, appropriate corrective action.
  • ADEA: age harassment of workers 40+ (employers with 20+ employees).
  • ADA: disability harassment (15+ employees). See our disability discrimination guide.

Sexual harassment can also take the form of a demand for sexual favors in exchange for job benefits. See quid pro quo sexual harassment.

3What Evidence Helps Prove It

  • A written log: dates, times, what was said or done, who was there
  • Texts, emails, chats and social media messages, saved before they disappear
  • Your complaints to HR or management, and their responses
  • Witnesses and coworkers who experienced the same thing
  • Medical or counseling records showing the impact on you

You don't need video. Be careful about taking confidential company documents; see recording and monitoring at work in Texas.

4Reporting to HR, and When HR Does Nothing

Reporting through your employer's complaint process matters. For coworker harassment, the employer is generally liable only if it knew or should have known and failed to act. For supervisor harassment, an employer can sometimes defend itself by showing the employee unreasonably failed to use its complaint procedure. Report in writing and keep a copy.

  • HR never follows up, or tells you to "work it out"
  • You get moved, written up or have hours cut after complaining
  • The harasser is promoted or the harassment gets worse

Know your rights in the process: HR investigations in Texas.

5Deadlines and What You Can Recover

Most harassment claims must start with a charge: generally 300 days with the EEOC, or 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission Civil Rights Division, which extends to 300 days for sexual harassment. See filing an EEOC claim. Possible remedies include back pay, front pay or reinstatement, compensatory and punitive damages (capped under federal law by employer size, from $50,000 to $300,000), and attorney's fees.

✅What to Do Now

  1. Write down every incident and save messages today.
  2. Read your handbook's harassment and complaint policy.
  3. Report in writing to HR or the person the policy names.
  4. Watch for retaliation and document any change after you complain.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer before deadlines run or before you resign.

?Frequently Asked Questions

What qualifies as a hostile work environment in Texas?

Harassment based on a protected characteristic, such as sex, race, religion, national origin, age, disability or pregnancy, that is severe or pervasive enough that a reasonable person would find the workplace intimidating, hostile or abusive.

Is a toxic workplace or a mean boss a hostile work environment?

Usually not by itself. Rudeness, yelling or unfair treatment is generally not illegal unless it is tied to a protected characteristic or is retaliation for protected activity.

Can one incident create a hostile work environment?

Yes, if it is severe enough, such as a physical or sexual assault or an extremely serious threat or slur.

Can coworkers create a hostile work environment?

Yes. Coworkers, customers and vendors can create a hostile work environment, and the employer can be liable if it knew or should have known and failed to take prompt corrective action.

How long do I have to file a harassment complaint in Texas?

Generally 300 days with the EEOC, or 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission. Sexual harassment complaints under Texas law can be filed with the TWC within 300 days.
