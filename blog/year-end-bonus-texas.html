Year-End Bonus in Texas: Can Your Employer Withhold It If You Quit or Get Fired?

Short answer

It depends on what was promised. Under the Texas Payday Law, a bonus is due according to the terms of the agreement between you and your employer. If a written plan, offer letter or policy promised a bonus based on set criteria and you met them, the employer generally must pay it, and you can file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when it was due. If the bonus is truly discretionary, or the plan clearly says you must still be employed on the payout date, an employer may be able to withhold it when you leave. A bonus can never be withheld for a discriminatory or retaliatory reason.

December is when Houston employees in energy, finance, healthcare and sales start counting on their annual bonus, and when some of them find out it isn't coming. Maybe you were laid off in November. Maybe you resigned for a new job in January, a week before payout. Whether you're owed that money comes down to the words in your bonus plan.

👉 The question isn't whether your boss "feels" you earned it. It's what the agreement says.

1Discretionary vs. Promised Bonuses

Promised (nondiscretionary)Tied to set criteria, like sales targets, production, a formula or a signed offer letter. Usually enforceable as wages.
DiscretionaryThe employer decides whether to pay it and how much, with no formula or promise. Usually hard to recover.
Retention or signing bonusControlled by the agreement, including any repayment ("clawback") terms if you leave early.
CommissionsAlso governed by the agreement. See how commissions are paid in Texas.

Calling a bonus "discretionary" in a handbook doesn't always settle it. If the company announced a formula, told you you'd earned it, or paid it the same way every year, the facts matter.

2The "Must Be Employed on the Payout Date" Rule

Many bonus plans say you must be employed when the bonus is paid. Under the Texas Payday Law, bonuses are due according to the terms of the agreement, so a clear, written condition like this is often enforced. Look closely at:

  • The exact wording: does it apply if you're laid off, or only if you resign or are fired for cause?
  • When it was added: was the condition in place when you earned the bonus?
  • Prorated payouts: some plans pay a partial bonus to employees laid off without cause.
  • Severance terms: a severance agreement may include or exclude your bonus. Read our guide to what a typical severance package includes.

3Final Pay Deadlines in Texas

Regular final wages have strict deadlines under the Texas Payday Law:

  • Fired or laid off: within 6 calendar days of discharge
  • Resigned: by the next regularly scheduled payday

Bonuses and commissions may follow a separate payout schedule if it is clearly defined in the written agreement, but they still must be paid on time under those terms.

4Bonuses and Overtime

For non-exempt employees, a nondiscretionary bonus (for production, attendance or performance targets) generally must be included in the "regular rate" used to calculate overtime. If you worked overtime during the bonus period, you may be owed additional overtime pay. See our overtime guide.

5When Withholding a Bonus Is Illegal

  • You were fired right before payout after complaining about harassment, discrimination or unpaid wages
  • Coworkers of a different race, sex or age with similar results got their bonuses
  • Your bonus was cut after you took FMLA or maternity leave in a way others' weren't
  • The company promised a bonus in writing and simply refused to pay

These can support claims for unpaid wages, discrimination or retaliation.

✅What to Do If Your Bonus Wasn't Paid

  1. Get the bonus plan, offer letter or policy in writing.
  2. Save proof you met the criteria: sales reports, reviews, emails saying you earned it.
  3. Ask for payment in writing and keep the response.
  4. Don't sign a severance agreement that waives your bonus without reviewing it. A severance attorney can help.
  5. File a TWC wage claim within 180 days of the date the bonus was due, or talk to a lawyer.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is a bonus considered wages in Texas?

Yes, when it was promised. The Texas Payday Law treats bonuses as wages that are due according to the terms of the agreement between the employee and employer. Truly discretionary bonuses are much harder to recover.

Can my employer withhold my bonus if I quit?

Possibly. If the written bonus plan clearly requires you to be employed on the payout date, the employer may be able to withhold it. If the bonus was earned under set criteria with no such condition, it may be owed.

Do I get my bonus if I'm laid off in Texas?

It depends on the plan. Some plans pay a full or prorated bonus to employees laid off without cause, while others require employment on the payout date. Check the plan and any severance agreement before signing.

How long do I have to file a wage claim for an unpaid bonus in Texas?

A Texas Payday Law wage claim must be filed with the Texas Workforce Commission no later than 180 days after the date the wages were originally due.

Does a bonus affect overtime pay?

Nondiscretionary bonuses generally must be included in a non-exempt employee's regular rate when calculating overtime, which can mean additional overtime is owed.
