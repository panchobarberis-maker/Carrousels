Put on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) in Texas? What It Means and How to Protect Yourself

Short answer

A performance improvement plan (PIP) is a written warning that sets goals and a deadline, usually 30 to 90 days, to improve your performance. No law requires Texas employers to use PIPs, and because most Texas employees are at-will, an employer can fire you during or after a PIP, or without one. But a PIP can't be used for an illegal purpose. If it appears right after you complained about discrimination, harassment or unpaid wages, requested medical leave or an accommodation, or disclosed a pregnancy, it may be evidence of retaliation or discrimination. Respond in writing, document everything, and remember that in Texas, poor performance alone is generally not "misconduct" that disqualifies you from unemployment benefits.

Few things at work feel as ominous as a meeting invite from your manager and HR, followed by a document titled "Performance Improvement Plan." Some PIPs are a genuine chance to improve. Others are a paper trail for a firing that's already been decided. Here's how to tell the difference, and what Texas employees can do.

👉 A PIP is a document. What matters is why it showed up, and what you put in writing next.

1What a PIP Means in an At-Will State

Texas doesn't regulate PIPs. Your employer decides whether to use one, how long it lasts and what goals it sets. In an at-will job, the employer can end your employment before the PIP ends, or even if you meet the goals, as long as the real reason isn't illegal. Learn more about at-will employment in Texas.

That's why the timing and fairness of a PIP matter so much. A PIP can become important evidence, for your employer or for you.

2Warning Signs a PIP Is a Setup

  • It arrived soon after you reported harassment, discrimination, unpaid overtime or a safety issue
  • It followed a request for FMLA leave, a disability accommodation or pregnancy-related changes
  • Your reviews were strong until recently, with no prior warnings
  • The goals are vague, impossible or moving ("improve attitude," targets no one on the team hits)
  • Coworkers of a different race, sex or age with the same numbers weren't put on a PIP
  • You're denied the training, tools or meetings the PIP promises

Any of these can support a claim for retaliation or discrimination. See also progressive discipline.

3Do You Have to Sign a PIP?

Signing usually means "received"Most PIP forms ask you to acknowledge receipt, not to agree that the criticism is fair.
Refusing can backfireAn at-will employer may treat refusing to sign as insubordination.
Add your commentsWrite "received, not agreed," and submit a written response with your side.
Keep a copyAsk for a copy of the signed PIP and any attachments.

4PIPs, Severance and Unemployment

  • Unemployment: the Texas Workforce Commission says inability to do the job is not, by itself, misconduct. Being fired after a failed PIP doesn't automatically disqualify you. See collecting unemployment if you were fired.
  • Severance: some employers offer a severance package instead of a PIP, or at the end of one. Read what a typical severance package includes before you sign.
  • "For cause": a failed PIP may be labeled cause under some severance or bonus plans. See termination for cause in Texas.

✅How to Respond to a PIP

  1. Stay calm in the meeting and ask questions about specific, measurable goals.
  2. Respond in writing within a few days with facts, not emotion, and correct any inaccuracies.
  3. Document your progress weekly and email your manager summaries.
  4. Write down the timeline: complaints, leave requests and how coworkers were treated.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer early, before the PIP ends or you sign any severance agreement.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Does a PIP mean I'm going to be fired?

Not always, but it is a serious warning. In an at-will state like Texas, an employer can fire an employee during or after a PIP, or without one, as long as the reason is not illegal.

Do I have to sign a performance improvement plan?

Signing usually only acknowledges that you received the PIP. Refusing can be treated as insubordination, so many employees sign "received, not agreed" and submit a written response.

Can a PIP be retaliation?

Yes. A PIP issued soon after you complained about discrimination, harassment or wages, requested leave or an accommodation, or disclosed a pregnancy can be evidence of illegal retaliation or discrimination.

Can I get unemployment if I'm fired after a PIP in Texas?

Possibly. The Texas Workforce Commission does not treat inability to do the job, by itself, as misconduct, and the employer generally must prove misconduct to disqualify you.

Is a PIP required before firing someone in Texas?

No. Texas law does not require PIPs or progressive discipline, although an employer's own policies and how consistently it follows them can matter in a discrimination or retaliation case.
