Can a Former Employer Give You a Bad Reference in Texas?

Short answer

Yes, if it's true. Texas employers can share truthful information about a former employee's job performance, and Chapter 103 of the Texas Labor Code gives them immunity from lawsuits over those disclosures. That immunity is lost if it's proven by clear and convincing evidence that the employer knew the information was false, or disclosed it with malice or reckless disregard for the truth. A false statement of fact that costs you a job can be defamation, and a negative reference given because you complained about discrimination or filed a charge can be illegal retaliation under federal law. No Texas law requires an employer to give a reference at all.

You've landed the interview, the offer looks close, and then the calls stop. Many Houston job seekers suspect that a former boss said something during a reference check. Some employers say nothing beyond dates and title. Others say too much. Here's where the law draws the line.

👉 Truth is generally protected. Lies and payback are not.

1What a Former Employer Can Legally Say

Generally allowedDates of employment, title, pay, eligibility for rehire, and honest opinions about performance or conduct.
Texas immunityEmployers are protected for disclosures about current or former employees unless they knew the information was false or acted with malice or reckless disregard.
No duty to give referencesTexas law doesn't require references. Many companies only confirm dates and title.
Neutral reference policiesMany companies send reference calls to HR and bar managers from commenting on performance.

2When a Bad Reference Becomes Defamation

Defamation is a false statement of fact communicated to someone else that harms your reputation. In a reference, examples might include:

  • Saying you were fired for theft or fraud when you weren't
  • Claiming you were terminated when you resigned
  • Saying you failed a drug test you never took

Opinions ("not a great fit") are usually not defamation, and the Texas immunity statute means you generally must show the employer knew it was false or acted with malice. Defamation claims in Texas generally must be filed within one year. For false statements made while you still work there, see defamation in the workplace.

3Retaliatory References Are Illegal

Federal anti-discrimination laws protect former employees too. If a former employer gives a negative reference, or tells a prospective employer about your complaint, because you:

  • Filed an EEOC or Texas Workforce Commission discrimination charge
  • Complained about harassment, discrimination or unequal pay
  • Participated in an investigation or lawsuit

that can be illegal retaliation, even if everything else they said was true. Learn about filing an EEOC claim.

4Protect Your Reference Before You Leave

  • Negotiate it in your severance agreement: ask for a neutral reference clause or an agreed reference letter. See what a typical severance package includes.
  • Ask HR for the official policy on what will be shared.
  • Line up your own references from supervisors who know your work.
  • If you were fired "for cause," read what termination for cause means.

✅If You Think a Reference Cost You a Job

  1. Ask the prospective employer whether a reference affected the decision. Some will tell you.
  2. Use a reference-checking service or a trusted contact to learn what your former employer says.
  3. Write down exactly what was said, by whom and when.
  4. Send a written request that your former employer stop making false statements.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer quickly. Defamation deadlines are short.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal for a former employer to give a bad reference in Texas?

Yes, if the information is truthful. Texas Labor Code Chapter 103 gives employers immunity for disclosures about current or former employees unless the employer knew the information was false or acted with malice or reckless disregard for the truth.

Do Texas employers have to give a reference?

No. Texas law does not require an employer to provide a reference about a current or former employee. Many employers only confirm dates of employment and job title.

Can I sue a former employer for a false reference?

Possibly, for defamation, if the employer made a false statement of fact that harmed you and knew it was false or acted with malice. Defamation claims in Texas generally must be filed within one year.

Can a former employer retaliate with a bad reference?

No. Giving a negative reference because a former employee filed a discrimination charge or complained about discrimination or harassment can be illegal retaliation under federal law.

Can I negotiate a neutral reference?

Yes. A neutral reference clause or an agreed reference letter can often be negotiated as part of a severance agreement.
