Jury Duty and Your Job in Texas: Can You Be Fired or Lose Pay for Serving?

Short answer

No, a Texas employer cannot fire a permanent employee for serving on a jury or grand jury. Under Chapter 122 of the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code, an employee fired for jury service is entitled to reinstatement and damages of at least one year's and up to five years' compensation, and must sue within two years of the date of service. Texas does not require private employers to pay hourly employees for time spent on jury duty, but salaried exempt employees generally cannot have their pay docked for jury service in a week they also work, although the employer may offset the jury fees they receive.

Harris County summons thousands of residents for jury service every week. For many Houston workers, the summons brings an immediate worry: will my boss hold this against me? Texas law answers clearly, and the penalties for employers who punish jurors are some of the strongest in Texas employment law.

1What Texas Law Protects

Section 122.001 of the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code says a private employer may not terminate a permanent employee because the employee serves as a juror or grand juror. If you're fired in violation of the law:

  • You're entitled to return to the same job you held when summoned, by giving your employer actual notice as soon as practical after you're released from jury service
  • You can sue for damages of at least one year's and up to five years' compensation at the rate you earned when summoned
  • You must file suit within two years of the date you served

An employer can defend itself only by proving the firing was for reasons other than jury service, or that its circumstances changed so much that reemployment was impossible or unreasonable.

2Does Your Employer Have to Pay You for Jury Duty?

Company policyIf a written policy promises paid jury leave, that pay can be claimed as wages.

3Other Ways Employers Cross the Line

  • Telling you to ignore the summons or lie to get excused
  • Threatening your job if you're selected for a long trial
  • Firing you shortly after you return, with a sudden new reason
  • Cutting your hours or giving you worse shifts because you served

If an employer claims a different reason for firing you right after jury service, the timing matters. Learn how to spot a pretext in our guide to termination for cause, and read about at-will employment in Texas.

✅What to Do When You're Summoned

  1. Give your employer a copy of the summons as soon as you receive it, and keep proof you did.
  2. Ask HR in writing whether jury days are paid under company policy.
  3. Keep your juror attendance records from the court.
  4. Notify your employer when you're released and return to work promptly.
  5. If you're fired or punished, talk to a lawyer quickly. The deadline is two years from your service.

Can I be fired for jury duty in Texas?

No. Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code Section 122.001 prohibits private employers from terminating a permanent employee because the employee serves as a juror or grand juror.

What can I recover if I was fired for jury service?

You may be entitled to reinstatement and damages of at least one year's and up to five years' compensation at the rate you earned when summoned.

Does my employer have to pay me while I'm on jury duty in Texas?

Texas law does not require private employers to pay hourly employees for jury duty. Salaried exempt employees generally cannot have their salary reduced for jury duty in a week they work, but the employer may offset jury fees received.

How long do I have to sue if I was fired for jury duty?

A lawsuit for damages must be filed no later than the second anniversary of the date you served as a juror or grand juror.

Does the law protect grand jurors?

Yes. The Texas protection covers both jury and grand jury service.
