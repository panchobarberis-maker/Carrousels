Can You Collect Unemployment If You Were Fired in Texas? (2026 Guide)

Short answer

Often, yes. Being fired doesn't automatically disqualify you from unemployment in Texas. The Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) denies benefits only if you were fired for misconduct connected with the work, such as intentional wrongdoing or violating a known safety or conduct rule, and the employer generally has to prove it. Poor performance or simply not being a good fit usually isn't misconduct. For claims starting on or after October 4, 2026, weekly benefits range from $76 to $619, for up to 26 weeks. If you're denied, you have 14 calendar days from the mailing date to appeal.

"Can I still get unemployment if they fired me?" is one of the first questions Houston employees ask after losing a job. Many don't apply because they assume a firing means no benefits. That's a mistake. In Texas, the reason you were fired, and whether your employer can prove it, decides your claim.

2What "Misconduct" Means in Texas

The Texas Labor Code defines misconduct to include mismanagement of a position through action or inaction, neglect that endangers life or property, intentional wrongdoing, intentional violation of a law, and violation of a policy or rule adopted to ensure orderly work and employee safety. TWC guidance adds three points that help many claimants:

  • The employer generally has the burden of proving misconduct.
  • TWC focuses on the final incident that caused the firing.
  • Inability to do the job is not misconduct by itself, and the employer must usually show you were warned.

An employer can call a firing "for cause" and you can still qualify. Read termination for cause in Texas and, if you were on a performance plan, what a PIP means.

3How Much You Can Receive in 2026

TWC calculates your weekly benefit amount from your highest-paid quarter in your base period. For claims effective on or after October 4, 2026, the weekly amount ranges from $76 to $619. Your maximum total benefit is the lesser of 26 times your weekly amount or 27% of your base period wages. Severance or pay in lieu of notice can delay when benefits start. See severance and unemployment in Texas.

4If Your Claim Is Denied: Appeal Within 14 Days

Don't miss the deadline

You have 14 calendar days from the mailing date on the determination to appeal. Keep requesting payment every two weeks while the appeal is pending.

  1. File the appeal online, in person, by mail or by fax, not by email or phone.
  2. Gather evidence: reviews, emails, texts, the termination letter, witness names.
  3. Prepare for the phone hearing: explain the final incident, any warnings, and why it wasn't misconduct.
  4. Ask the employer's witnesses questions; secondhand statements carry less weight.

5After You Apply: Work Search Rules

  • Register for work search at WorkInTexas.com or a Workforce Solutions office within three business days of applying, and keep it active.
  • Request payment every two weeks and report any earnings.
  • Log your work search activities and submit them if TWC asks.

6When the Firing Itself Was Illegal

Unemployment is only part of the picture. If you were fired after complaining about harassment or discrimination, requesting medical leave, reporting a safety hazard, or refusing to do something illegal, you may also have a legal claim. Can I get unemployment if I was fired in Texas?

Often, yes. Texas denies benefits only when you were fired for misconduct connected with the work. The employer generally must prove misconduct, and poor performance alone usually does not count.

What is considered misconduct for unemployment in Texas?

The Texas Labor Code defines misconduct to include mismanagement of a position, neglect that endangers life or property, intentional wrongdoing, intentional violation of a law, and violating a rule adopted to ensure orderly work and employee safety.

How much is unemployment in Texas in 2026?

For claims effective on or after October 4, 2026, weekly benefits range from $76 to $619, depending on your past wages, for up to 26 weeks.

How long do I have to appeal an unemployment denial in Texas?

You have 14 calendar days from the mailing date on the determination to file an appeal with the Texas Workforce Commission.

Does severance affect unemployment in Texas?

Severance pay or wages in lieu of notice can delay when unemployment benefits begin, but they generally do not cancel benefits.
