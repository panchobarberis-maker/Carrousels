Just Fired in Texas? What to Do in the First 48 Hours

Short answer

Don't sign anything on the spot, especially a severance agreement or release. Write down exactly what happened while it's fresh, save documents you're allowed to keep, and confirm your final paycheck, which is due within six calendar days in Texas when you're fired or laid off. Apply for unemployment with the Texas Workforce Commission right away, because being fired doesn't automatically disqualify you. Check your health insurance options, ask what your employer will tell future employers, and watch the deadlines: discrimination charges generally must be filed within 180 days with the TWC or 300 days with the EEOC.

Losing a job is a shock, and the first two days are when people make the mistakes that are hardest to undo: signing a release under pressure, deleting messages, missing a deadline, or assuming that "at-will" means they have no rights. Here's a step-by-step checklist from a Houston employment law firm, with links to our detailed guides on each issue.

👉 You can't undo a signature. Take 48 hours to understand what you're giving up.

1Don't Sign Anything Yet

Many employers hand over a severance agreement in the termination meeting. It almost always includes a release of legal claims. You're usually allowed time to review it, and if you're 40 or older and part of a group layoff, federal law generally gives you 45 days to consider it and 7 days to revoke.

Read what a typical severance package includes and why to have a severance attorney review it before you sign.

2Write Down What Happened

  1. Who was in the meeting and what reason they gave
  2. Recent events: complaints you made, leave you requested, reviews, a PIP
  3. Who else was let go, and who wasn't
  4. Save documents you're permitted to keep: offer letter, reviews, pay stubs, the termination letter

If you were recently put on a performance plan, see what a PIP means in Texas. If they said you were fired "for cause," read termination for cause in Texas.

3Check Your Final Paycheck

Under the Texas Payday Law, final pay is due within 6 calendar days if you're fired or laid off. Unused vacation is owed only if a written policy or agreement provides for it. Bonuses and commissions follow the terms of your plan. See whether a bonus can be withheld.

4Apply for Unemployment Now

File with the Texas Workforce Commission as soon as possible. Being fired disqualifies you only if it was for misconduct connected with the work, the employer generally has to prove it, and poor performance alone usually isn't misconduct. Severance or pay in lieu of notice can delay when benefits start. Read collecting unemployment if you were fired.

5Health Insurance, References and Your Next Job

Health coverageIf your employer offered a group plan, you may be able to continue it under COBRA. Watch the election deadline in the notice you receive.
ReferencesAsk what will be shared. See what a former employer can legally say.
Non-competeCheck whether you signed one before accepting a new job.
Company propertyReturn it promptly, and ask for a written receipt.

6Signs Your Firing May Have Been Illegal

Texas is at-will, but a firing is illegal if the real reason was discrimination, retaliation or another protected reason. Warning signs:

  • You were fired soon after complaining about harassment, discrimination, unpaid wages or safety
  • You were fired after requesting leave, an accommodation, or disclosing a pregnancy
  • You refused to do something illegal. See the Sabine Pilot exception.
  • The reason given keeps changing, or coworkers who did the same thing kept their jobs

Learn more about retaliation in the workplace and at-will employment in Texas.

Deadlines

Discrimination charges generally must be filed within 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission or 300 days with the EEOC. Wage claims with the TWC must be filed within 180 days of when the wages were due.

?Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do right after being fired in Texas?

Don't sign anything immediately, write down what happened, save documents you're allowed to keep, confirm your final pay, apply for unemployment and talk to an employment lawyer about whether the firing was legal.

When do I get my final paycheck if I'm fired in Texas?

Under the Texas Payday Law, final pay is due within six calendar days of being fired or laid off.

Can I get unemployment if I was fired?

Often, yes. Texas disqualifies claimants fired for misconduct connected with the work, but the employer generally must prove misconduct, and poor performance alone usually is not misconduct.

Should I sign a severance agreement right away?

No. Severance agreements usually include a release of legal claims. Take the time you're given to review it, and have an employment lawyer look at it before signing.

How long do I have to file a discrimination claim after being fired in Texas?

Generally 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission or 300 days with the EEOC from the date of the firing.
