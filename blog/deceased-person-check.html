How to Cash a Check Made Out to a Deceased Person in Texas

Short answer

In Texas, a check made out to someone who has died belongs to their estate, so a family member can't simply sign it and deposit it. The usual way is for the court-appointed executor or administrator to deposit it into an estate bank account opened with letters testamentary or letters of administration and an estate EIN. For smaller estates without a will, heirs may be able to collect it with a court-approved small estate affidavit (estate worth $75,000 or less, excluding the homestead and exempt property). A power of attorney ends at death, so the former agent can't cash it. Social Security payments for the month of death or later must be returned.

A refund, a final paycheck, an insurance check: the mail keeps coming after a loved one dies. Banks see these checks every day, and most will refuse them unless the right person brings the right documents. Here's who can cash the check, and how.

1Who Can Cash or Deposit the Check

Executor or administratorAppointed by the probate court and given letters testamentary or letters of administration. Deposits the check into an estate account.
Heirs with a small estate affidavitFor estates without a will worth $75,000 or less, excluding the homestead and exempt property, once a judge approves the affidavit.
A co-payeeIf the check is payable to two people, the bank's rules decide whether the survivor can deposit it, often with a death certificate.
Not the power of attorneyUnder Texas law a durable power of attorney ends when the principal dies. See power of attorney after death.

If a will exists, it may be probated as a muniment of title without an executor; some banks accept the court order to release funds. When there's no will, an affidavit of heirship may help identify the heirs.

2Common Checks After a Death, and What to Do

  • Social Security: benefits aren't payable for the month of death. A check for that month or later must be returned, and direct deposits should be sent back through the bank.
  • Federal tax refund: a surviving spouse filing jointly or the estate's representative can claim it; others generally file IRS Form 1310.
  • Final paycheck, refunds and insurance checks: usually estate assets, deposited into the estate account or collected through a small estate affidavit.
  • Life insurance or retirement accounts with a named beneficiary: paid directly to the beneficiary, outside probate.

3Where the Money Goes

Once someone has authority, the check goes into an account in the estate's name, not a family member's account. The executor or administrator handles that step; see why an executor can't deposit an estate check into a personal account. If you don't know which banks the person used, see how to find a deceased person's bank accounts.

4Mistakes That Cause Problems

  • Signing the deceased person's name on the check, which can be treated as forgery
  • Using the old power of attorney after the death
  • Cashing a Social Security check for the month of death
  • Depositing into a personal or joint account without authority, which can create disputes with other heirs
  • Waiting too long: banks may refuse checks older than six months

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can I cash a check made out to a deceased person in Texas?

Only with legal authority. The executor or administrator can deposit it into an estate account, or heirs can collect it with a court-approved small estate affidavit if the estate qualifies.

Can a power of attorney cash a check after death?

No. Under Texas Estates Code Section 751.131, a durable power of attorney terminates when the principal dies, so the agent loses authority to handle the person's money.

What do I do with a Social Security check after someone dies?

Return it. Social Security is not payable for the month of death, so any check for that month or later must be sent back, and direct deposits should be returned through the bank.

Can I deposit a deceased person's check into their own bank account?

Usually not. Once the bank learns of the death, the account is typically restricted until someone shows legal authority, and joint or payable-on-death accounts pass to the surviving owner or beneficiary. Checks payable to the deceased should go into an estate account opened by the executor or administrator.

What if the estate is small?

If there is no will and the estate is worth $75,000 or less, excluding the homestead and exempt property, heirs may use a small estate affidavit after 30 days, once a judge approves it.
