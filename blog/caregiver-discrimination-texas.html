Caregiver Discrimination in Texas: Treated Differently Because You Care for a Child or Parent?

Short answer

Being a caregiver is not, by itself, a protected category under federal or Texas law. But caregiver discrimination is often illegal anyway. Title VII prohibits decisions based on sex stereotypes, such as assuming mothers are less committed or refusing fathers the leave mothers get. The ADA prohibits discrimination because of your association with a family member who has a disability. And the FMLA gives eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave to care for a child, spouse or parent with a serious health condition, with protection against retaliation for using it.

You were passed over for a promotion right after your manager learned you were caring for your mother. You were told the new role "needs someone without small kids." You were fired after asking for time off for your son's surgery. These situations happen every day in Houston workplaces, and many of them break federal law, even though "caregiver" isn't a word you'll find in the statutes.

👉 The law doesn't protect "caregivers" by name. It protects you from the stereotypes and retaliation that caregivers face.

1Sex Stereotyping Under Title VII

The EEOC's guidance on workers with caregiving responsibilities explains that treating a caregiver worse because of assumptions about gender is sex discrimination. Examples:

  • A mother is denied a promotion or travel assignment because the manager assumes she won't want it
  • A pregnant employee or new mother is moved to a lesser role "for her own good"
  • A father is denied parental or caregiving leave that mothers routinely receive
  • Comments like "she's not as committed since the baby" appear in reviews or meetings

Treating all parents equally badly is not sex discrimination, but treating mothers and fathers differently can be. Learn more about gender discrimination in Texas.

2Caring for a Family Member with a Disability (ADA)

The Americans with Disabilities Act makes it illegal for employers with 15 or more employees to discriminate against you because of your association with someone who has a disability, such as a child with autism or a parent with dementia. It is illegal to:

  • Refuse to hire you because they fear you'll miss work to care for a disabled relative
  • Fire you because a family member's medical costs raise the company's health insurance
  • Treat you worse than coworkers based on assumptions about your caregiving
Know the limit

The ADA's duty to provide reasonable accommodations, like a schedule change, applies to employees with their own disability. It doesn't require an employer to accommodate your caregiving schedule, but it does require you be held to the same rules as everyone else.

3FMLA Leave to Care for Family

The Family and Medical Leave Act gives eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year to care for a spouse, child or parent with a serious health condition, or to bond with a new child. You're generally eligible if:

Employer size50 or more employees within 75 miles of your worksite
Time on the jobYou've worked there at least 12 months
Hours workedAt least 1,250 hours in the past 12 months
Military caregiversUp to 26 weeks to care for a covered servicemember

It is illegal to interfere with FMLA leave or retaliate against you for taking it. See our FMLA guide for Texas and whether you can be fired while on FMLA leave.

4Pregnancy and New Parents

Pregnancy discrimination is sex discrimination under Title VII, and the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires reasonable accommodations for pregnancy, childbirth and related conditions. New mothers also have the right to break time and a private space to pump at work. Texas has no state paid family leave law; read more about maternity leave in Texas.

✅Signs You May Have a Claim, and What to Do

  1. Write down comments about your kids, your parent's health or your "commitment," with dates and witnesses.
  2. Compare treatment: who got the promotion, schedule or leave you were denied, and do they have caregiving duties?
  3. Request leave in writing and keep copies of medical certifications.
  4. Track timing: did your reviews drop after you disclosed caregiving or asked for leave?
  5. Watch deadlines: discrimination charges generally must be filed within 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission or 300 days with the EEOC.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is caregiver discrimination illegal in Texas?

Caregiver status is not a protected category by itself, but caregiver discrimination is often illegal under Title VII when based on sex stereotypes, under the ADA when based on association with a family member with a disability, and under the FMLA when it involves leave.

Can I be fired for taking care of a sick parent?

If you are eligible for FMLA leave, your employer cannot fire you for taking protected leave to care for a parent with a serious health condition. If you are not eligible, you may still be protected if the firing was based on your association with a parent who has a disability.

Can my employer refuse to promote me because I have kids?

If the decision is based on stereotypes about mothers or fathers, such as assuming a mother won't travel, it may be sex discrimination under Title VII.

Does my employer have to change my schedule so I can care for my child?

Generally no. The ADA's accommodation duty covers your own disability, not a family member's. But your employer cannot treat you worse than coworkers because of your caregiving or sex, and FMLA leave may be available.

How much FMLA leave can I take to care for family?

Eligible employees can take up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period, or up to 26 weeks to care for a covered servicemember.
