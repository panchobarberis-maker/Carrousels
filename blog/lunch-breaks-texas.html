Lunch and Rest Breaks in Texas: Does Your Employer Have to Give You a Break?

Short answer

No. Neither federal law nor Texas law requires private employers to give adult employees lunch or rest breaks. But if your employer offers breaks, federal rules decide whether they're paid: short breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes count as hours worked and must be paid, while a bona fide meal period, ordinarily 30 minutes or more, can be unpaid only if it's really your time and not spent mainly for the employer's benefit. If you work through lunch, answer calls at your desk, or your employer automatically deducts a meal break you didn't take, that time generally must be paid, including overtime. Separate laws require break time for nursing employees and may require breaks as a religious or disability accommodation.

Many Houston workers, from nurses and warehouse crews to restaurant staff and call-center agents, are surprised to learn there's no general right to a lunch break in Texas. The real money question is different: are you getting paid for the breaks you do take, and for the ones you don't?

1Are Breaks Required in Texas?

The U.S. Department of Labor confirms that the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) does not require meal or rest breaks. Texas has no state law requiring breaks for adult employees in the private sector. Whether you get breaks, and how long, is generally up to your employer's policy.

2Which Breaks Must Be Paid?

Short breaks (5–20 min)Coffee, restroom or smoke breaks count as hours worked and must be paid.
Meal periods (usually 30+ min)Can be unpaid if the time is really yours. You don't have to be allowed to leave the building.
Working while eatingAnswering phones, watching a counter or monitoring patients during "lunch" makes it work time.
OvertimePaid breaks count toward the 40 hours that trigger overtime for non-exempt employees.

Federal courts in Texas ask whether a meal period is spent predominantly for the employer's benefit. If you're on call, constantly interrupted or required to stay at your station, the time is likely compensable. See also when on-call time must be paid.

3Automatic Meal Deductions and "Working Through Lunch"

Many hospitals, plants and warehouses automatically deduct 30 minutes a shift for lunch. That's legal only if you actually get the break. Warning signs of unpaid wages:

  • Your time system auto-deducts a meal break you regularly miss or cut short
  • You're told to clock out and keep working
  • You eat at your desk while answering calls or emails
  • Your lunch is interrupted so often it's never really yours

Over months, a missed 30-minute break per shift can add up to significant unpaid overtime. Read whether an employer can refuse to pay for unauthorized overtime and our overtime guide.

4Breaks the Law Does Protect

  • Nursing employees: the federal PUMP Act requires reasonable break time and a private space to pump for one year after birth. See pumping at work in Texas.
  • Religious practice: employers with 15 or more employees must reasonably accommodate prayer breaks unless it causes undue hardship. See religion in the workplace.
  • Medical needs: extra breaks for diabetes, pregnancy or other conditions can be a reasonable accommodation under the ADA or the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act.

✅If You're Not Being Paid for Breaks

  1. Keep your own log of missed or interrupted meal breaks.
  2. Save pay stubs and time records showing automatic deductions.
  3. Report missed breaks in writing so payroll can correct them.
  4. Ask for a schedule accommodation in writing if you need breaks for medical, religious or nursing reasons.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer about recovering unpaid wages and overtime.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Are lunch breaks required by law in Texas?

No. Neither federal law nor Texas law requires private employers to provide lunch or rest breaks to adult employees. Break policies are set by the employer.

Do short breaks have to be paid in Texas?

Yes. Under federal rules, short breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes are counted as hours worked and must be paid, and they count toward overtime.

Can my employer make me work through lunch without pay?

No. If you work during a meal period, or the break is spent mainly for the employer's benefit, that time is generally compensable and must be paid, including overtime when you exceed 40 hours.

Is an automatic lunch deduction legal?

Only if you actually receive the break. If your employer automatically deducts a meal period you miss or work through, the deducted time may be unpaid wages.

Do I have a right to breaks to pump breast milk?

Yes. The federal PUMP Act requires most employers to provide reasonable break time and a private space other than a bathroom to pump for up to one year after a child's birth.
