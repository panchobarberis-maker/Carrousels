Hurricanes and Your Job in Houston: Pay, Safety and Getting Fired for Evacuating

Short answer

If a hurricane closes your workplace in Houston, hourly (non-exempt) employees generally only have to be paid for hours actually worked. Salaried exempt employees must receive their full weekly salary if the business closes for less than a full workweek, although the employer can require them to use paid time off. Texas has no general law protecting private employees who miss work to evacuate, but you may be protected if you refuse work that poses an imminent danger, need disability-related or FMLA leave, or were treated differently than coworkers for a discriminatory reason.

Houston knows what a storm does to a paycheck. After Hurricane Harvey and Hurricane Beryl, thousands of workers were left asking the same questions: Will I get paid while the office is closed? Can my boss make me come in? Can I lose my job for leaving town? Atlantic hurricane season runs through November 30, so here are the answers before the next storm.

👉 Your rights depend on how you are paid and why you missed work.

1Will I Get Paid If Work Closes for a Hurricane?

The federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) treats hourly and salaried workers differently:

Hourly / non-exemptGenerally paid only for hours actually worked. If the business closes and you don't work, the employer usually doesn't have to pay you, unless a contract, union agreement or company policy says otherwise.
Salaried exemptIf the employer closes for less than a full workweek, you must receive your full salary for that week. No deduction for closure days, though the employer may require you to use PTO.
Office open, you stay homeIf the business stays open and an exempt employee chooses not to come in, the employer may deduct for full-day absences only, never partial days.
Remote work during the stormIf you answer emails or take calls from home, that is work. Hourly employees must be paid for that time.
Your regular payday still applies

Under the Texas Payday Law, wages already earned must be paid on the regular payday, even if the office is closed after a storm.

2Can My Employer Make Me Come to Work During a Hurricane?

Texas is an at-will state, and no Texas law gives private employees a general right to stay home during a storm. Employers can usually require essential staff to work. But some protections still apply:

  • Imminent danger: under OSHA rules, you may refuse a task if you reasonably believe it poses an imminent risk of death or serious injury, you asked the employer to fix the hazard, and there was no time to report it through normal channels.
  • Acting together: the National Labor Relations Act protects employees who raise safety concerns as a group, union or not.
  • Disability: if a medical condition makes travel or work during the storm dangerous, you can request a reasonable accommodation under the ADA.

3Can I Be Fired for Evacuating?

Possibly. Without a contract or a specific legal protection, a Texas employer can discipline or fire an at-will employee for missing work, even during a mandatory evacuation. A firing may still be illegal if:

  • You were caring for a family member with a serious health condition and qualified for FMLA leave
  • You needed time off for your own disability or medical condition
  • Coworkers of a different race, sex, age or national origin missed the same days and were not fired
  • You were fired for complaining about unsafe conditions or unpaid wages

If one of these applies, you may have a wrongful termination claim.

4Unemployment and Disaster Assistance

If a storm shuts down your employer and you are out of work, you can apply for unemployment benefits with the Texas Workforce Commission. When the President declares a major disaster, workers who don't qualify for regular benefits, including many self-employed and gig workers, may be eligible for Disaster Unemployment Assistance (DUA). DUA has short filing deadlines after the declaration, so apply quickly. See also our guide to unemployment benefits in Texas.

✅Before the Next Storm

  1. Read your employer's emergency or inclement-weather policy and save a copy.
  2. Know whether you are exempt or non-exempt. It decides whether closure days are paid.
  3. Get instructions in writing: if your manager tells you to come in or stay home, ask for a text or email.
  4. Track every hour worked remotely during the storm.
  5. If you are disciplined or fired, write down who else missed work and how they were treated.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Does my employer have to pay me if it closes for a hurricane in Texas?

Hourly employees generally only have to be paid for hours worked. Salaried exempt employees must receive their full weekly salary if the business closes for less than a full workweek, although the employer may require them to use PTO.

Can my employer force me to use vacation days for a hurricane closure?

Generally yes. Texas law does not prohibit employers from requiring employees to use accrued paid time off for closure days, as long as exempt employees still receive their full salary.

Can I be fired for not coming to work during a hurricane in Houston?

Texas is an at-will state, so it is possible. A firing may be illegal if it involved FMLA-protected leave, a disability, discrimination, or retaliation for raising safety or pay complaints.

Can I refuse to work if conditions are dangerous?

Under OSHA rules, you may refuse a task if you reasonably believe it poses an imminent danger of death or serious injury, you asked your employer to fix it, and there is no time to use normal reporting channels.

Can I get unemployment if a hurricane closes my job?

You can apply with the Texas Workforce Commission. After a federal disaster declaration, Disaster Unemployment Assistance may also be available to workers who don't qualify for regular benefits.
