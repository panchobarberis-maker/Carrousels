Exit Interview in Texas: Do You Have to Do One, and What Should You Say?

Short answer

No Texas or federal law requires you to do an exit interview, and your final paycheck can't be held hostage to one. While you're still employed, an at-will employer can ask you to attend, but you can keep your answers short and professional. Nothing you say is confidential, so stick to facts and avoid insults. If you're leaving because of harassment, discrimination, unpaid wages or retaliation, an exit interview is a chance to put it on the record, and reporting it is protected activity. Don't sign a release, severance agreement or "resignation" form on the spot; take it home and read it. Return company property, ask about final pay and benefits, and keep copies of everything.

Your last day, a meeting with HR and a form asking, "Why are you leaving?" Most people either say nothing or say too much. An exit interview can help you, or create a record that's used against you later. Here's how to handle it.

2What to Say, and What to Avoid

  • Give a simple reason: a new opportunity, pay, schedule, or "I'd prefer not to go into detail."
  • Stick to facts if you raise a problem: dates, what happened, who was involved.
  • Name the legal issue plainly, such as "sexual harassment" or "unpaid overtime," if that's why you're leaving.
  • Insults, venting about coworkers, or threats
  • Saying you quit "for no reason" if you're really leaving because of illegal treatment
  • Admitting to policy violations or guessing about facts you don't know
  • Agreeing on the spot to non-compete, confidentiality or repayment terms

3Reporting Harassment or Discrimination on the Way Out

Complaining about harassment, discrimination or retaliation is protected activity, even in an exit interview. Retaliation can include a bad reference or a "not eligible for rehire" note made because you complained. Put your complaint in writing and keep a copy. If you were pushed out, your resignation may still be treated as a firing for some purposes. Read hostile work environment in Texas and retaliation in the workplace. Discrimination charges generally must be filed within 180 days with the TWC or 300 days with the EEOC.

4Paperwork, Pay and Benefits

  • Releases and severance: don't sign in the meeting. See severance lawyer in Texas.
  • Final pay: owed within six calendar days if you were fired, or by the next regular payday if you quit, whether or not you do the interview.
  • Unemployment: if you quit for good cause connected with the work, document why. What you tell HR can come up at TWC. See unemployment if you quit.
  • Benefits: ask about COBRA, your 401(k), unused PTO under company policy, and when coverage ends.
  • Company property: return it and get a receipt; don't take confidential files.

✅Exit Interview Checklist

  1. Decide your one-sentence reason before the meeting.
  2. Bring notes if you plan to report a problem, and follow up in an email.
  3. Ask for copies of anything you're asked to sign.
  4. Confirm final pay, benefits and your last day in writing.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer first if you may have a claim or were offered severance. See two weeks' notice in Texas.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to do an exit interview in Texas?

No law requires it. An employer can ask while you are still employed, and an at-will employer has wide discretion, but your final paycheck cannot be conditioned on completing one.

Is what I say in an exit interview confidential?

No. The employer can share and use what you say, so keep it factual and professional.

Should I mention harassment or discrimination in my exit interview?

If that is why you are leaving, reporting it factually can create a record and is protected activity. Consider talking to a lawyer first if you may have a claim.

Can my employer give me a bad reference because of my exit interview?

Not in retaliation for complaining about discrimination, harassment or other protected issues. Truthful references are otherwise generally allowed in Texas.

Should I sign documents at my exit interview?

Don't sign a release, severance agreement or new restrictive agreement on the spot. Ask for a copy and time to review it.
