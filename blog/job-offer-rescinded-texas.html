Job Offer Rescinded in Texas? Can an Employer Take It Back?

Short answer

Usually yes. In Texas, most jobs are at-will, so an employer can generally withdraw a job offer before or even after you accept it, unless you have a contract for a set term. But it can't rescind an offer for an illegal reason, such as your race, sex, pregnancy, age, religion, national origin or disability, or because you asked for an accommodation. If the offer is pulled because of a background check, the employer must follow the Fair Credit Reporting Act notice steps; if it's pulled because of a post-offer medical exam, the reason must be job-related and consistent with business necessity. If you quit or relocated in reliance on the offer, you may have a limited claim, so save every message.

You accepted, told your boss you were leaving, maybe even started packing for Houston. Then an email: "We've decided to go in a different direction." A rescinded offer feels like it should be illegal. Often it isn't, but the reason and the timing can change that.

2Background Checks, Drug Tests and Medical Exams

  • Background check: if a report from a background check company is the reason, you must get a copy of the report and a summary of your rights before the decision is final, then a final notice. See background checks in Texas.
  • Drug test: offers are often contingent on a test. See drug testing at work in Texas.
  • Medical exam: after a conditional offer, an employer can require a medical exam if all new hires in the same job get one. It can withdraw the offer based on the results only if you can't do the essential functions even with a reasonable accommodation, or would pose a direct safety threat.
  • References: a bad or false reference can sink an offer; see employer references in Texas.

3If You Already Quit or Moved

Texas courts are split here. At least one Houston appeals court allowed a promissory estoppel claim to go forward when an applicant resigned and prepared for an overseas assignment in reliance on an offer that was then revoked (Roberts v. Geosource Drilling Services, 1988), while other Texas courts have not followed that approach. When such a claim works, it's usually limited to out-of-pocket losses caused by relying on the promise, not years of lost salary. Ask your old employer whether you can stay or come back, and apply for unemployment if you're out of work; TWC decides eligibility based on the facts. See unemployment if you quit.

4Questions to Ask Before You Give Notice

  • Is the offer contingent on a background check, drug test, references or a medical exam, and are those complete?
  • Is there a signed offer letter with a start date, pay and title?
  • Does the offer include a non-compete or arbitration agreement? See are non-competes enforceable in Texas.
  • Will the employer cover relocation, and what happens to that money if the offer is withdrawn?

For timing your resignation, see two weeks' notice in Texas.

✅What to Do If Your Offer Was Withdrawn

  1. Ask for the reason in writing; they don't have to give one, but the answer matters.
  2. Save everything: the offer letter, emails, texts and notes from calls.
  3. Write a timeline: when you disclosed a pregnancy, condition, age or complaint, and when the offer was pulled.
  4. Request the background report if one was used.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer before discrimination deadlines run.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can an employer rescind a job offer after I accepted in Texas?

Generally yes. Most Texas jobs are at-will, so an employer can withdraw an offer even after acceptance, unless the reason is illegal or there is a contract for a set term.

Is it illegal to rescind a job offer because I'm pregnant?

Yes. Withdrawing an offer because of pregnancy is sex discrimination under federal and Texas law for employers with 15 or more employees.

Can I sue if I quit my job for an offer that was rescinded?

Possibly. Some Texas courts have allowed promissory estoppel claims for losses caused by relying on a job offer, but results vary and damages are usually limited to out-of-pocket losses.

Can an employer withdraw an offer after a background check?

Yes, but if it relies on a report from a background check company, it must first give you a copy of the report and a summary of your rights, then a final adverse action notice.

Can an employer withdraw an offer after a medical exam?

Only if the reason is job-related and consistent with business necessity, such as being unable to perform essential functions even with a reasonable accommodation.
