Should You Take a Voluntary Buyout? What Houston Employees Need to Know Before Saying Yes

Short answer

A voluntary buyout is a severance payment offered in exchange for resigning and, almost always, signing a release of legal claims. Before you accept, compare the offer to what you would receive if you were laid off later, check how it affects retirement, health coverage and unemployment, and read the release. If you are 40 or older and the buyout is offered to a group, federal law gives you at least 45 days to decide, 7 days to revoke after signing, and information about who was eligible.

In February 2026, Memorial Hermann announced a Voluntary Severance Program for eligible non-clinical employees as part of a two-and-a-half-year strategic plan, citing changes in ACA subsidies, Medicaid funding and rising costs. Hospitals, energy companies and government contractors across Houston use buyouts to reduce headcount without layoffs.

👉 A buyout is voluntary. That is exactly why you should read every line before you accept it.

1What a Voluntary Buyout Really Is

A buyout (also called a voluntary separation, voluntary severance or early retirement incentive) is an offer: you leave your job, and the employer pays you money or benefits. In return, you usually sign an agreement that includes:

  • A release of claims: you give up the right to sue the employer, including for discrimination or unpaid wages
  • A resignation date and, sometimes, a requirement to keep working until then
  • Confidentiality, non-disparagement and sometimes non-compete or non-solicitation terms

Because you are giving up legal rights, the agreement is a contract. Learn what to look for in a typical severance package.

2Over 40? You Get 45 Days and Information

The Older Workers Benefit Protection Act (OWBPA) applies to exit incentive programs offered to a group, not just layoffs. If you are 40 or older and asked to waive age-discrimination claims, the employer generally must:

  • Give you at least 45 days to consider the offer
  • Give you 7 days after signing to revoke
  • Tell you in writing who was eligible for the program, the eligibility factors, the time limits, and the job titles and ages of eligible and ineligible employees

If those requirements are not met, the waiver of age claims may not be valid. Learn more about age discrimination in Texas.

3Five Questions to Answer Before You Decide

  1. How does the buyout compare to severance if you are laid off later? Ask HR whether layoffs are planned and what the standard severance formula is.
  2. What happens to your retirement? Check pension and 401(k) vesting dates, early-retirement eligibility and whether the offer adds service credit.
  3. How long does health coverage last? Find out when your plan ends and whether COBRA premiums are subsidized.
  4. Is it paid as a lump sum or over time? Lump sums are taxed as supplemental wages and can push you into a higher bracket for that year.
  5. What claims are you releasing? If you have a pending complaint, an injury or unpaid wages, the release may wipe them out.

4Can You Collect Unemployment After a Buyout in Texas?

It depends. Accepting a buyout can look like a voluntary resignation, and the Texas Workforce Commission generally denies benefits to people who quit without good cause connected to the work. The facts matter, especially whether your job was actually going to be eliminated. Severance payments can also delay when benefits begin.

Read more about unemployment after quitting and severance and unemployment in Texas.

5Can You Negotiate a Buyout?

Group programs often use a fixed formula, but individual terms can sometimes change: the release date, the scope of a non-compete, a neutral reference, the treatment of bonus or equity, or extra pay when there are potential legal claims. A severance attorney can tell you what is realistic.

If you say no

Declining a buyout is your right. If you are later laid off or treated worse for refusing, and the reason involves your age or another protected characteristic, that may be illegal.

?Frequently Asked Questions

What is a voluntary buyout?

A voluntary buyout is an offer of severance pay or benefits in exchange for resigning, usually conditioned on signing a release of legal claims against the employer.

How long do I have to decide on a buyout offer?

The offer sets the deadline, but if you are 40 or older and the buyout is offered to a group, federal law generally requires at least 45 days to consider it and 7 days to revoke after signing.

Can I get unemployment in Texas if I take a buyout?

It depends on the facts. Accepting a buyout may be treated as a voluntary quit, which usually disqualifies you, but whether your job was actually being eliminated can matter. Severance can also delay when benefits start.

Is a voluntary buyout better than being laid off?

Not always. Compare the buyout to the severance you would likely receive in a layoff, and consider retirement vesting, health coverage and unemployment eligibility before deciding.

Can I be punished for turning down a buyout?

Declining is your right. If an employer retaliates or targets you for layoff because of your age or another protected characteristic after you decline, you may have a legal claim.
