Severance Lawyer in Texas: What to Know Before You Sign

Short answer

Texas law doesn't require employers to pay severance, so most severance offers are a trade: money or benefits in exchange for you releasing legal claims against the company. That makes the offer negotiable, especially if you may have a discrimination, retaliation, unpaid wage or leave claim. A severance lawyer reviews what you're giving up, spots unfair terms like broad non-competes, and negotiates the amount, health coverage, bonuses and references. If you're 40 or older, federal law generally gives you 21 days to consider the agreement (45 in a group layoff) and 7 days to revoke after signing. Your final paycheck is owed whether or not you sign.

The meeting is short, the papers are already printed, and HR wants a signature by Friday. A severance agreement is often the last chance to negotiate with your employer, and the release inside it usually means you can't sue later. Here's what to check, and what you can ask for.

1What You Give Up When You Sign

  • A release of claims: discrimination, retaliation, wrongful termination and often contract claims
  • Confidentiality and non-disparagement terms limiting what you can say
  • Non-compete or non-solicitation limits on your next job; see are non-competes enforceable in Texas
  • Cooperation clauses requiring you to help the company in future disputes
  • Repayment of severance if you break a term

Some rights can't be signed away. A severance agreement can't stop you from filing a charge with the EEOC or taking part in its investigation, even if you've waived your right to recover money personally.

3When a Severance Lawyer Helps Most

  • You think the firing was linked to age, race, sex, pregnancy, disability, a complaint, medical leave or an injury
  • You're owed overtime, commissions or bonuses
  • The offer is low for your years of service, or others got more
  • The agreement has a non-compete or a broad confidentiality clause
  • You're being pressured to sign on the spot

Potential legal claims are the main source of negotiating leverage. Without them, a lawyer can still catch costly terms and ask for reasonable improvements.

4Workers 40 and Older: Extra Protections

Under the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act, a release of age discrimination claims is valid only if it's written in plain language, specifically mentions the ADEA, advises you in writing to consult a lawyer, gives you 21 days to consider (or 45 days in a group layoff or exit program, along with information about who was selected) and 7 days to revoke after signing, and gives you something beyond what you were already owed.

5What You're Owed Even If You Don't Sign

Under the Texas Payday Law, a fired or laid-off employee must receive final pay within six calendar days; an employee who quits, by the next regular payday. Earned wages aren't something an employer can hold hostage for a signature. Unemployment is decided by TWC, not by the agreement. If you were just let go, see what to do in the first 48 hours; if it was part of a closure, layoffs when a company closes.

✅Before You Sign

  1. Don't sign in the meeting. Ask for the deadline in writing.
  2. Collect documents: offer letter, bonus or commission plans, reviews, emails about the firing.
  3. Write down why you think you were let go and any complaints you made.
  4. List what matters: money, health insurance, references, freedom to work.
  5. Have an employment lawyer review it before the deadline.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is severance pay required in Texas?

No. Texas law does not require employers to offer severance. It is owed only if a contract, policy or plan promises it, or if it is offered in exchange for a release.

Can I negotiate a severance agreement?

Often, yes. Employees can negotiate the amount, health coverage, bonuses, references and restrictive terms, especially when they may have legal claims against the employer.

How long do I have to sign a severance agreement?

Your employer sets the deadline, but workers 40 and older generally must get 21 days to consider an agreement that waives age claims, or 45 days in a group layoff, plus 7 days to revoke after signing.

Can I sue my employer after signing a severance agreement?

Usually not for the claims you released. That is why it is important to understand your potential claims before signing. You can still file an EEOC charge, but you may not be able to recover money personally.

Do I have to sign to get my final paycheck?

No. Under the Texas Payday Law, earned final wages are owed regardless; fired employees must be paid within six calendar days.
