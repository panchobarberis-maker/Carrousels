Military Leave and USERRA in Texas: Your Job Rights as a Service Member or Veteran

Short answer

Under the federal Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA), employers of every size must give unpaid leave for military service and reemploy you when you return, as long as you gave advance notice, your cumulative service with that employer is generally five years or less, you were not discharged under other-than-honorable conditions, and you report back on time. Returning service members are protected from being fired without cause for one year if they served more than 180 days, or for 180 days if they served 31 to 180 days. Employers also cannot deny hiring, promotion or benefits because of past, current or future military service.

Houston is home to thousands of National Guard members, reservists and veterans, many of them balancing drill weekends, annual training and deployments with civilian jobs. Every year some come home to find their job gone, their position "restructured," or a manager who isn't happy about the next set of orders. Federal law gives you strong protection, and it applies to employers of every size.

1Who Is Protected

USERRA protects employees who serve in the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Coast Guard and Space Force, their Reserves, the Army and Air National Guard (for federal training or duty), and the commissioned corps of the Public Health Service. It covers active duty, drills, annual training, fitness-for-duty exams and funeral honors duty.

To keep reemployment rights you generally must:

  • Give your employer advance notice of service (written or verbal), unless military necessity prevents it
  • Stay within the five-year cumulative limit with that employer (training, drills and many mobilizations don't count)
  • Not be separated with a disqualifying discharge
  • Return or apply on time (see below)

3What Your Employer Owes You When You Return

  • The "escalator" position: the job, pay and seniority you would have reached if you hadn't left, not just your old job
  • Training to requalify if needed
  • Pension credit for the time you served, as if you had kept working
  • Health coverage: you can elect to continue employer coverage during service (up to 24 months), and coverage must be reinstated on return without new waiting periods
  • Disability accommodations for service-connected injuries

Military leave can be unpaid, but you can choose to use accrued vacation or PTO. Your employer can't force you to.

4Protection From Being Fired

After reemployment, you can't be discharged except for cause:

  • For one year, if your service lasted more than 180 days
  • For 180 days, if your service lasted 31 to 180 days

The employer has the burden of proving the discharge was reasonable. Service of 30 days or less doesn't get this "for cause" period, but you're still protected from discrimination and retaliation because of your service at any time.

  • Not hired or promoted because "you might get deployed"
  • Bad reviews or reduced hours after you announce orders
  • Pressure to choose between your unit and your job
  • Retaliation for filing a USERRA complaint or testifying

Family members caring for a service member may also qualify for up to 26 weeks of military caregiver leave under the FMLA. See also caregiver discrimination.

✅How to Enforce Your Rights

  1. Keep copies of your orders and the notice you gave your employer.
  2. Apply for reemployment in writing within the deadline and keep proof.
  3. Contact ESGR (Employer Support of the Guard and Reserve) for free informal mediation.
  4. File a complaint with the U.S. Department of Labor's VETS office, or
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer about filing suit. USERRA has no statute of limitations, but acting quickly preserves evidence.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can I be fired for military service in Texas?

No. USERRA prohibits employers of any size from firing, refusing to hire or denying promotions or benefits because of military service, and requires reemployment after qualifying service.

Does my employer have to pay me during military leave?

No. Military leave under USERRA can be unpaid, but you may choose to use accrued vacation or paid time off. Your employer cannot require you to use it.

How long do I have to return to work after military service?

For service of 1 to 30 days, report at the next work period after safe travel plus 8 hours of rest. For 31 to 180 days, apply within 14 days. For 181 days or more, apply within 90 days.

Can I be fired after returning from deployment?

After service of more than 180 days, you can only be fired for cause for one year after reemployment. After 31 to 180 days of service, that protection lasts 180 days.

Where do I file a USERRA complaint?

You can contact ESGR for informal mediation, file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Labor's Veterans' Employment and Training Service, or bring a lawsuit with a private attorney.
