Can Your Employer Make You Take a Lie Detector Test in Texas?

Short answer

Usually not. The federal Employee Polygraph Protection Act (EPPA) bars most private employers from requiring or even requesting a lie detector test from employees or job applicants, and from firing, disciplining or refusing to hire someone for refusing one or based on the results. There are narrow exceptions: polygraph tests during an ongoing investigation of a specific economic loss, such as theft, if strict conditions are met, and pre-employment tests for certain security firms and companies that handle controlled substances. Government employers aren't covered. You can sue within three years of a violation for reinstatement, lost wages and benefits.

Cash shortages, missing inventory, a stolen laptop: when something goes wrong at work, some managers reach for a "lie detector" to find out who did it. In most private Houston workplaces, that's against federal law, and so is punishing an employee who says no.

1What the Law Prohibits

For most private employers, the Employee Polygraph Protection Act makes it illegal to:

  • Require, request, suggest or cause an employee or applicant to take a lie detector test
  • Use, accept or ask about the results of any lie detector test
  • Fire, discipline, discriminate against or refuse to hire someone for refusing a test, or based on test results
  • Retaliate against someone for filing a complaint or asserting their rights under the law

"Lie detector" includes polygraphs and similar devices. The limited exceptions below apply only to polygraphs.

2The Narrow Exceptions

Ongoing investigationOf a specific workplace incident causing economic loss, like theft or embezzlement, for employees who had access and are reasonably suspected.
Security servicesApplicants for certain armored car, alarm and security guard jobs.
Controlled substancesApplicants and employees of pharmaceutical firms with direct access to controlled substances.
GovernmentFederal, state and local government employers aren't covered.
Even in an investigation

The employer must give you a written statement about the incident and why you're suspected before the test, and it can't fire or discipline you based only on the polygraph results or your refusal. It needs other supporting evidence.

3Your Rights If a Test Is Allowed

  • You can stop the test at any time.
  • You must get written notice in advance, including the time and place, and your right to consult a lawyer.
  • The examiner can't ask about religion, politics, union activity, race or sexual behavior.
  • Results are generally confidential.

✅If You're Asked to Take a Lie Detector Test

  1. Ask for the request in writing, including the reason.
  2. Ask which exception the employer is relying on.
  3. Don't sign anything until you understand it, and talk to a lawyer if you can.
  4. Write down what happens if you decline: who said what, and when.
  5. File within three years if you're fired or punished. You can also contact the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer make me take a polygraph test in Texas?

Usually not. The federal Employee Polygraph Protection Act prohibits most private employers from requiring or requesting lie detector tests, with narrow exceptions for certain investigations, security firms and companies that handle controlled substances.

Can I be fired for refusing a lie detector test?

For most private employers, no. It is illegal to fire, discipline or refuse to hire someone for refusing a lie detector test or based on its results.

When can an employer use a polygraph during an investigation?

Only for an ongoing investigation of a specific incident causing economic loss, for employees who had access and are reasonably suspected, after giving a written statement. The employer cannot act based only on the results or a refusal.

Do government employees have these protections?

The Employee Polygraph Protection Act does not cover federal, state or local government employers.

How long do I have to sue for a lie detector violation?

A lawsuit must be filed within three years of the violation. Remedies can include reinstatement, promotion, and lost wages and benefits.
