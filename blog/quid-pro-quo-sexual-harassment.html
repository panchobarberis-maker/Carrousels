Quid Pro Quo Sexual Harassment in Texas: What It Is and What to Do

Short answer

Quid pro quo ("this for that") sexual harassment happens when someone with power over your job, usually a supervisor, conditions a job benefit or threatens a job consequence on your submitting to sexual advances: a promotion for a date, or a firing after you say no. It's illegal under Title VII and Texas Labor Code Chapter 21, and since 2021 Texas sexual harassment law covers employers with even one employee. It doesn't require physical contact or an explicit threat. When a supervisor's harassment results in a tangible job action like firing or demotion, the employer is generally liable. In Texas, you can file with the Texas Workforce Commission within 300 days, or with the EEOC generally within 300 days.

"Go to dinner with me and that raise is yours." "If you're not going to be friendly, maybe this job isn't for you." Quid pro quo harassment is an abuse of power, and it's one of the clearest forms of illegal sexual harassment. Here's how to recognize it and protect yourself.

1Common Examples

PromiseA manager offers a promotion, raise or better shifts in exchange for a date or sexual favors.
ThreatA supervisor hints you'll be fired, demoted or lose hours if you refuse.
PunishmentAfter you say no, you suddenly get bad reviews, worse assignments or a termination.
"Joking"Repeated "jokes" about what it would take to get ahead, from someone who decides your future.

2Who Can Commit It, and Who Can Be a Victim

  • The harasser is usually someone with authority over your job: a supervisor, manager, owner, or someone who influences hiring, pay or schedules. A coworker without that power more often creates a hostile work environment instead.
  • Anyone can be a victim, of any sex, and the harasser can be the same sex.
  • Saying yes under pressure doesn't make it consensual or legal; the question is whether the advances were unwelcome.

3Quid Pro Quo vs. Hostile Work Environment

Quid pro quoA job benefit or consequence is tied to submitting to sexual conduct. One clear demand can be enough.
Hostile work environmentHarassment severe or pervasive enough to make the workplace abusive, from anyone, including coworkers.

Many cases involve both. Also see sexual coercion and the law in Texas.

4Evidence and Employer Liability

  • Save every text, DM, email and voicemail; don't delete anything, even embarrassing messages
  • Write down conversations right away: date, words used, who was nearby
  • Keep reviews and pay records from before and after you said no; a sudden change is powerful evidence
  • Tell someone you trust and report in writing to HR

When a supervisor's harassment ends in a tangible employment action, like a firing, demotion or loss of pay, the employer is generally liable. Under Texas law, an employer is also liable for sexual harassment if it, its agents or supervisors knew or should have known and failed to take immediate and appropriate corrective action.

5Retaliation After You Say No or Report

Being written up, moved, cut or fired after rejecting advances or reporting harassment can be both harassment and illegal retaliation. If you've already been let go, read what to do in the first 48 hours after being fired, and know your rights in an HR investigation.

✅What to Do Now

  1. Preserve the evidence on your own device or account, not only on a work phone.
  2. Report in writing under your employer's harassment policy, if it's safe to do so.
  3. Track any change in pay, schedule or reviews after you said no or reported.
  4. Watch the deadlines: 300 days for TWC sexual harassment complaints; generally 300 days with the EEOC. See filing an EEOC claim.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer before you resign or sign anything.

?Frequently Asked Questions

What is quid pro quo sexual harassment?

It is when a job benefit, such as a promotion or raise, or a job consequence, such as firing or demotion, is conditioned on submitting to unwelcome sexual advances or conduct.

Is quid pro quo harassment illegal in Texas?

Yes. It violates Title VII and Texas Labor Code Chapter 21. Since 2021, Texas sexual harassment law applies to employers with one or more employees.

Does quid pro quo harassment require physical contact?

No. Words, messages, promises and threats can be enough. Physical contact is not required.

What if I said no and was fired?

A firing after rejecting a supervisor's advances can be quid pro quo harassment and retaliation, and the employer is generally liable when a supervisor's harassment results in a tangible employment action.

How long do I have to file a sexual harassment complaint in Texas?

You can file with the Texas Workforce Commission within 300 days of the harassment, and with the EEOC generally within 300 days in Texas.
