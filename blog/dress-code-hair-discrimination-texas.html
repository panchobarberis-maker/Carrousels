Dress Codes and Hair Discrimination in Texas: What the CROWN Act Protects

Short answer

Texas employers can generally set dress and grooming codes, but not discriminatory ones. Since September 1, 2023, the Texas CROWN Act (Labor Code § 21.1095) has made it illegal race discrimination for an employer to adopt or enforce a dress or grooming policy that discriminates against a hair texture or protective hairstyle commonly or historically associated with race, including braids, locks and twists. Employers with 15 or more employees must also accommodate religious dress and grooming, such as head coverings or beards, unless it causes undue hardship, and may need to accommodate disability-related needs. A discrimination charge generally must be filed within 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission or 300 days with the EEOC.

"Your locs aren't professional." "Braids aren't allowed on the sales floor." "Cut your hair or you can't start Monday." For years, Black employees and job applicants in Houston heard versions of these comments with little legal recourse. Texas changed that in 2023. Here's what an employer can and can't require about how you look at work.

1What the Texas CROWN Act Says

House Bill 567 added Section 21.1095 to the Texas Labor Code. It makes it an unlawful employment practice for an employer, labor union or employment agency to adopt or enforce a dress or grooming policy that discriminates against a hair texture or protective hairstyle commonly or historically associated with race. The law names braids, locks and twists as protective hairstyles.

  • Refusing to hire someone because of locs or braids
  • Requiring Black employees to straighten or cut natural hair
  • Disciplining an employee for twists under a "neat hair" policy
  • Keeping someone off customer-facing work because of their hairstyle

The law applies to conduct on or after September 1, 2023. Learn more about race discrimination in Texas.

2Religious Dress and Grooming

Under Title VII, employers with 15 or more employees must reasonably accommodate religious dress and grooming practices unless doing so causes undue hardship, which the Supreme Court has said means substantial increased costs. The Supreme Court has also held that an employer can't refuse to hire an applicant to avoid accommodating a religious practice it suspects, such as a headscarf, even if the applicant never asked.

3Sex-Based Dress Codes and Disability

  • Sex-based rules: different dress standards for men and women can be lawful, but rules that impose heavier burdens on one sex, or rely on sex stereotypes, can be discrimination. See gender discrimination.
  • Disability: medical conditions may require exceptions, such as shaving rules for employees with skin conditions or footwear for foot problems, as a reasonable accommodation.
  • Pregnancy: uniform changes during pregnancy can be an accommodation under the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act.

✅If a Dress or Grooming Rule Targets You

  1. Get the written dress code and any messages about your hair or clothing.
  2. Write down comments about your hairstyle, attire or "professional" appearance, with dates and witnesses.
  3. Request a religious or medical accommodation in writing if that's the reason for your appearance.
  4. Compare treatment: are others with different hair or attire held to the same rule?
  5. Watch the deadline: 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission or 300 days with the EEOC. See filing an EEOC claim.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is hair discrimination illegal in Texas?

Yes. Since September 1, 2023, the Texas CROWN Act makes it illegal race discrimination for an employer to adopt or enforce a dress or grooming policy that discriminates against a hair texture or protective hairstyle commonly or historically associated with race.

What hairstyles does the Texas CROWN Act protect?

The law protects hair textures and protective hairstyles commonly or historically associated with race, and specifically names braids, locks and twists.

Can my employer have a dress code in Texas?

Yes. Employers can set dress and grooming codes, but they cannot apply them in a way that discriminates based on race, religion, sex, disability or another protected characteristic.

Does my employer have to allow religious clothing or a beard?

Employers with 15 or more employees must reasonably accommodate religious dress and grooming practices unless doing so would cause undue hardship, meaning substantial increased costs.

How long do I have to file a hair discrimination complaint in Texas?

A charge generally must be filed within 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission Civil Rights Division or within 300 days with the EEOC.
