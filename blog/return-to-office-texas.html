Return-to-Office Mandates in Texas: Can You Be Fired for Refusing, and When Is Remote Work Protected?

Short answer

In most cases, yes. Texas is an at-will state, and unless a contract or written agreement promises remote work, a private employer can generally require you to return to the office and fire you if you refuse. The main exception is disability: under the Americans with Disabilities Act, an employer with 15 or more employees must consider remote work as a possible reasonable accommodation through an individualized interactive process, and a general return-to-office policy does not erase that duty. A mandate is also illegal if it is enforced selectively based on race, sex, age, pregnancy or another protected characteristic, or used to retaliate against you.

From energy companies downtown to banks, hospitals and the federal government, Houston employers are calling people back to their desks. For many employees, the mandate is just an inconvenience. For others, it's a disability, a pregnancy or a caregiving problem, and the question becomes: do I have to go back, or can I lose my job?

👉 Preferring to work from home is not protected. Needing to because of a disability can be.

1The Starting Point: Texas Is At-Will

Most Texas employees work at will. An employer can change where you work, your schedule or your duties, and can end employment for any reason that isn't illegal. That means a return-to-office (RTO) order is usually lawful, and refusing it can be treated as job abandonment or insubordination.

Check first whether anything in writing changes that:

  • An offer letter or employment contract that says the job is remote
  • A remote-work agreement you signed with HR
  • A union contract covering work location
  • A previously approved disability accommodation

2Remote Work as a Disability Accommodation (ADA)

The Americans with Disabilities Act applies to employers with 15 or more employees. The EEOC has long said that working from home can be a reasonable accommodation, even if the employer doesn't let other employees telework. What the law requires:

A real requestTell your employer that a medical condition makes it hard to work on-site. You don't need legal words.
The interactive processThe employer must discuss your limitations and options with you, not just point to the RTO policy.
Essential functionsRemote work is not required if the job's essential duties truly must be done on-site.
Effective, not preferredThe employer can choose another accommodation that works, like a private office, a modified schedule or hybrid days.

Read more about when a Texas employer can deny a reasonable accommodation and examples of ADA failure-to-accommodate violations.

3What the EEOC's 2026 Telework Guidance Says

In February 2026, the EEOC and the Office of Personnel Management issued FAQs on telework accommodations for federal employees returning to the office. They are not binding on private employers, but they show how the EEOC is likely to look at these disputes:

  • A return-to-office order can justify re-evaluating an existing telework accommodation, but each request still needs an individualized assessment.
  • An employer may ask you to try an in-office accommodation first. If it doesn't work, telework should be considered again.
  • A long commute, a preference, or general symptom relief alone usually does not require full-time telework.
  • Working remotely during COVID did not permanently change a job's essential functions.
Mental health counts too

Anxiety, depression, PTSD and other mental health conditions can qualify as disabilities. See ADA and mental health at work.

4When an RTO Mandate Becomes Illegal

Even when the policy itself is lawful, how it's applied can break the law. Warning signs include:

  • Coworkers of a different race, sex or age keep working remotely while you are ordered back
  • The mandate targets you soon after you reported harassment, discrimination or unpaid wages
  • You asked for an accommodation and HR refused to discuss it because "the policy applies to everyone"
  • You are pregnant and were denied a temporary remote or hybrid arrangement under the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act without real consideration

Any of these can support a claim for disability discrimination, other discrimination or retaliation.

Deadlines are short

Discrimination charges generally must be filed within 180 days with the Texas Workforce Commission or 300 days with the EEOC. Learn about filing an EEOC claim.

5If You Quit or Are Fired: Unemployment

If you are fired for refusing to return, the Texas Workforce Commission will look at whether that was misconduct. If you quit, you generally must show good cause connected with the work, such as a major change in your job's terms or a medical reason you discussed with your employer first. The facts matter, so document everything before you decide. Read more about getting unemployment after you quit.

✅Before You Refuse to Go Back

  1. Find your paperwork: offer letter, remote agreement, past accommodation approvals.
  2. If you have a medical reason, request an accommodation in writing and ask for the interactive process.
  3. Get a note from your doctor describing your limitations, not just a diagnosis.
  4. Consider alternatives you could accept, such as hybrid days or a modified schedule.
  5. Don't simply stop showing up. Talk to an employment lawyer before the deadline.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer force me to return to the office in Texas?

Generally yes. Texas is an at-will state, so unless a contract or written agreement promises remote work, a private employer can require employees to work on-site and can fire employees who refuse.

Can remote work be a reasonable accommodation under the ADA?

Yes. Employers with 15 or more employees must consider remote work as a possible accommodation for a disability through an interactive process, but they can choose another effective accommodation or require on-site work if it is an essential function of the job.

Does a return-to-office policy override my accommodation?

No. A general return-to-office policy does not remove the employer's duty to assess accommodation requests individually, although it can justify re-evaluating an existing telework arrangement.

Can I get unemployment if I quit over a return-to-office mandate?

Possibly, but it is difficult. The Texas Workforce Commission generally requires good cause connected with the work. A medical reason or a major change in your job's terms can help, especially if you raised it with your employer first.

Is it discrimination if only some employees must return?

It can be. If an employer applies the mandate differently based on race, sex, age, disability, pregnancy or another protected characteristic, or uses it to retaliate against a complaint, that may be illegal.
