On-Call Time and Training Pay in Texas: When Waiting Counts as Work

Short answer

It depends on how much control your employer has over your time. Under federal law, which applies in Texas, on-call time is paid work if you must stay at the workplace or so close by, or respond so often, that you can't use the time for yourself. If you only need to keep your phone on and can otherwise go about your day, the waiting time generally isn't paid, but any time spent actually working on a call is. Training is paid work unless it's outside normal hours, truly voluntary, not directly related to your job, and you do no productive work. For non-exempt employees, all of this time counts toward overtime after 40 hours.

Refinery operators, IT staff, nurses, maintenance techs, tow drivers and property managers across Houston carry an on-call phone. Many are told on-call time is "just part of the job." Sometimes that's true. Sometimes it's unpaid work, and over a year it can add up to thousands of dollars in overtime.

👉 The question is simple: whose time is it, yours or your employer's?

1"Engaged to Wait" vs. "Waiting to Be Engaged"

Engaged to wait (paid)Your employer requires you to wait: a technician waiting at the site for parts, a driver waiting to be loaded. That's work.
Waiting to be engaged (unpaid)You're completely off duty, can leave and use the time for yourself, and are told when to come back.

2When On-Call Time Must Be Paid

Federal regulations say an employee who must remain on the employer's premises, or so close that they can't use the time effectively for their own purposes, is working while on call. An employee who only has to leave word where they can be reached is not. Factors that make on-call time more likely to be paid:

  • You must respond in a very short time (for example, be on site in 15 minutes)
  • You get frequent calls that interrupt personal activities
  • You can't leave a small geographic area, drink or trade shifts
  • You must stay at or near the workplace

Time spent actually responding, including phone calls, remote fixes and driving to a callout, is generally work time either way.

3Is Training Time Paid?

Under federal rules, training, lectures and meetings are not work time only if all four conditions are met:

  1. It's outside your regular working hours
  2. Attendance is truly voluntary
  3. It's not directly related to your job
  4. You do no productive work during it

Mandatory orientation, safety training, required certifications for your current job and online modules you're told to complete at home are usually paid work.

4How Unpaid Time Becomes Unpaid Overtime

For non-exempt employees, compensable on-call, callback and training time is added to the workweek. If it pushes you over 40 hours, it must be paid at time-and-a-half. Salaried doesn't always mean exempt

Being paid a salary doesn't automatically exempt you from overtime. Your duties and pay level decide. See the overtime exemption rule.

✅If You're Not Paid for On-Call or Training Time

  1. Save the on-call policy: response times, distance limits, call frequency.
  2. Log every call and callout: when, how long, what you had to do.
  3. Keep records of required training: emails, certificates, completion screens.
  4. Compare your pay stubs to your total hours.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer about unpaid wages and overtime.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Do I get paid for being on call in Texas?

It depends on the restrictions. If you must stay at or near the workplace, or the restrictions and frequency of calls keep you from using the time for yourself, on-call time is work and must be paid. If you only need to be reachable, waiting time generally is not paid.

Is time spent answering calls while on call paid?

Yes. Time spent actually working during an on-call shift, such as handling calls, remote work or responding to a callout, is generally compensable work time.

Does my employer have to pay me for training?

Generally yes. Training is unpaid only if it is outside regular hours, voluntary, not directly related to your job, and involves no productive work.

Is mandatory orientation paid?

Yes. Required orientation and job-related training are generally hours worked and must be paid.

Does on-call time count toward overtime?

Compensable on-call, callback and training time counts toward the 40 hours that trigger overtime for non-exempt employees.
