Furloughed in Texas? Pay, Unemployment and Your Rights During a Temporary Layoff

Short answer

A furlough is a temporary, usually unpaid, break from work or reduction in hours while you stay employed. In Texas, an at-will employer can generally furlough employees, but pay rules still apply: hourly employees must be paid for every hour they actually work, and salaried exempt employees must receive their full weekly salary for any week in which they do any work. You may qualify for unemployment benefits during a furlough, including partial benefits if your hours are cut and you earn less than 125% of your weekly benefit amount, or benefits under an employer's TWC Shared Work plan. A furlough that lasts more than six months can count as a layoff under the federal WARN Act.

Energy companies cutting rigs, hospitals trimming non-clinical staff, manufacturers slowing production: when Houston employers need to save money without permanent layoffs, they often turn to furloughs and reduced schedules. A furlough keeps your job on paper, but it can quickly drain your paycheck. Here's what employers can and can't do, and how to protect your income.

1Furlough vs. Layoff

FurloughTemporary unpaid time off or reduced hours. You're still an employee and may keep benefits, depending on the plan.
LayoffYour job ends. Final pay is due within 6 days under the Texas Payday Law.
Reduced scheduleFewer hours or days per week, often with a pay cut announced in advance.
"Indefinite" furloughIf there's no return date, treat it like a layoff and plan accordingly.

If the furlough turns into a permanent layoff, see what to do in the first 48 hours and mass layoffs in Texas.

2Pay Rules During a Furlough

  • Hourly (non-exempt) employees: must be paid for all hours actually worked, including emails, calls or "quick tasks" on furlough days.
  • Salaried exempt employees: under Department of Labor rules, must receive their full weekly salary for any week in which they perform any work. No pay is required only for a full workweek with no work at all.
  • Prospective pay cuts: a bona fide, announced reduction in salary going forward can be lawful; week-to-week deductions based on how busy the business is are not.
  • Accrued leave: employers can sometimes require you to use PTO, but exempt employees still must get their full salary for any week they work.

Don't work "off the books"

If you're told to stay unpaid but keep answering emails, track that time. For exempt employees, even a little work in a week can mean the full week's salary is owed.

3Unemployment During a Furlough

  • Full furlough: if you're not working at all, you can apply for unemployment with the Texas Workforce Commission.
  • Reduced hours: you may get partial benefits if your hours are cut and you earn less than 125% of your weekly benefit amount.
  • Shared Work plans: some employers use a TWC-approved Shared Work plan that reduces hours 10% to 40%, with workers receiving a matching share of unemployment benefits.

4When a Furlough Triggers WARN Act Rights

The federal WARN Act generally requires 60 days' notice before a plant closing or mass layoff at covered employers with 100 or more employees. A layoff that lasts more than six months, or a reduction in hours of more than 50% in each month of a six-month period, can count as an employment loss. If a "temporary" furlough stretches on without notice, you may be owed back pay and benefits. See our guide to the WARN Act in Texas.

5When a Furlough Is Discriminatory

  • Furlough selections that fall mostly on older workers, women or one racial group
  • Being furloughed right after complaining about pay, harassment or safety
  • Being furloughed after requesting medical leave or disclosing a pregnancy

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer furlough me in Texas?

Generally yes. Texas is an at-will state, so employers can usually furlough employees or reduce hours, but they must still follow wage laws and cannot choose who to furlough for discriminatory or retaliatory reasons.

Do I get paid during a furlough?

Hourly employees must be paid for hours actually worked. Salaried exempt employees must receive their full weekly salary for any week in which they do any work; no pay is required only for a full week with no work.

Can I collect unemployment if I'm furloughed in Texas?

Often yes. Fully furloughed workers can apply with the Texas Workforce Commission, and workers with reduced hours may qualify for partial benefits if they earn less than 125% of their weekly benefit amount, or through a Shared Work plan.

What is the TWC Shared Work program?

It is a voluntary program that lets employers reduce employees' hours by 10% to 40% instead of laying them off, with workers receiving a proportional share of unemployment benefits.

Does a long furlough count as a layoff under the WARN Act?

It can. A layoff that lasts more than six months, or a reduction in hours of more than 50% in each month of a six-month period, can count as an employment loss under the WARN Act.
