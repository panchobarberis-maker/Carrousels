Can You Be Fired for Having a Second Job in Texas?

Short answer

Yes, it's possible. Texas has no law protecting employees who work a second job, so an at-will employer can limit or ban outside work through a policy and discipline or fire employees who break it, especially when the side job competes with the company, uses its time or equipment, or creates a conflict of interest. Without such a policy, a second job alone is rarely a problem as long as it doesn't affect your work. Two separate employers generally don't have to combine your hours for overtime. If you're on FMLA leave, an employer can restrict outside work only through a uniformly applied written policy. And you must report side income to TWC or the workers' comp insurer if you're receiving benefits.

Rideshare on weekends, a restaurant shift at night, freelance design on the side. Many Houston workers need a second income. The question is whether your main employer can stop you, and what the side job means for your overtime, leave and benefits.

2Overtime When You Work Two Jobs

  • Two separate employers: each counts only its own hours, so 30 + 30 hours usually means no overtime from either.
  • Same employer, two roles: hours are combined, and anything over 40 in a workweek is overtime for non-exempt employees.
  • Related companies: if two businesses are joint employers, hours may have to be combined.

3Second Jobs During Leave or on Benefits

  • FMLA leave: the FMLA doesn't ban outside work, but an employer can enforce a written, uniformly applied policy against it. Work that contradicts your medical restrictions can raise questions about the leave. See our FMLA guide.
  • Workers' comp: earnings from another job generally must be reported, and physical work beyond your restrictions can hurt your claim. See workers' comp retaliation in Texas.
  • Unemployment: if you lose your main job but keep a part-time one, you may still qualify for partial benefits, but you must report all earnings to TWC.

4Your Schedule and Your Employer's Rights

A Texas employer can generally change your schedule even if it conflicts with your second job, unless a contract says otherwise. See can my boss change my schedule. Off-duty activities, including what you post online about a side business, can also lead to discipline in an at-will job; see fired for a social media post in Texas.

✅How to Protect Yourself

  1. Check the handbook and any non-compete, confidentiality or conflict-of-interest agreement.
  2. Ask for approval in writing if the policy requires it.
  3. Keep the jobs separate: no side work on company time, devices or with company clients.
  4. Track your hours at each job to spot unpaid overtime.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer if you're fired and others with side jobs weren't.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer fire me for having a second job in Texas?

Yes, it can if you violate a policy against outside work, work for a competitor, or the second job affects your performance. Texas has no law protecting a second job, although selective enforcement can be evidence of discrimination or retaliation.

Do I have to tell my employer about a second job?

Only if a policy or agreement requires it. Many handbooks require disclosure or approval, especially for jobs in the same industry.

Do two jobs add up for overtime?

Not if the employers are separate. Each employer counts only its own hours. Hours are combined if both jobs are for the same employer or for joint employers.

Can I work a second job while on FMLA leave?

The FMLA does not prohibit it, but an employer can enforce a written, uniformly applied policy against outside employment during leave.

Can I get unemployment if I lose one of two jobs?

Possibly. You may qualify for partial unemployment benefits from TWC if you lose your main job, but you must report all earnings from the job you keep.
