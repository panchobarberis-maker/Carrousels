Under HR Investigation in Texas? Your Rights in a Workplace Investigation

Short answer

In Texas, private employers can investigate employees and generally require you to cooperate, and refusing can lead to discipline in an at-will job. You usually don't have a right to bring a lawyer into an HR interview, but union-represented employees can ask for a union representative in an interview they reasonably believe could lead to discipline (Weingarten rights). Texas law doesn't give private employees a right to see their personnel file, though OSHA injury and illness records must be made available. If you reported harassment or discrimination, or participate in an EEOC proceeding, retaliating against you for it is illegal. Stay calm, be truthful, take notes afterward, and put your side in writing.

An HR meeting invite with no agenda. A request to "talk about a complaint." A sudden suspension "pending investigation." Whether you're the person who complained, a witness, or the one being accused, a workplace investigation can decide your job. Here's what Texas employees can and can't do.

1Your Role Changes Your Risks

You complainedReporting harassment or discrimination is protected. Watch for retaliation after the investigation.
You're a witnessAnswer truthfully about what you saw. Participating in an EEOC proceeding is protected by law.
You're accusedAsk what the allegations are, respond to each one, and give names of witnesses and documents.
Suspended "pending investigation"Ask whether it's paid and how long it will last, and get it in writing.

2Can You Bring Someone to the Interview?

  • A lawyer: private employers generally don't have to allow your attorney in an internal HR meeting. You can still talk to a lawyer before and after.
  • A union representative: union-represented employees can request one in an investigatory interview they reasonably believe may lead to discipline. If you ask, the employer must allow it, end the interview, or let you choose whether to continue without one.
  • Recording: Texas is a one-party consent state, but recording against a company policy can get you disciplined. See workplace recording and monitoring.

3Your Personnel File

Texas law doesn't require private employers to let employees view or copy their personnel files, although many do by policy. Exceptions:

  • OSHA records: employees and former employees can access OSHA injury and illness records.
  • Medical information: under the ADA, it must be kept confidential and separate from the regular personnel file.
  • Public employees: can request their records under the Texas Public Information Act.

Ask in writing for copies of any write-ups, your PIP or documents you're asked to sign.

4When the Investigation Is Used Against You

  • You reported harassment, and you ended up disciplined or transferred
  • The investigation started right after you complained about pay, safety or discrimination
  • Witnesses you named were never interviewed
  • Others accused of the same thing were treated more leniently

Those can be signs of retaliation. If the investigation ends in a firing, see what to do in the first 48 hours.

✅How to Handle an HR Interview

  1. Stay calm and truthful. Dishonesty in an investigation is a common firing reason.
  2. Ask what the allegations are and answer each one specifically.
  3. Name witnesses and documents that support your side.
  4. Write down what was asked and answered right after the meeting.
  5. Follow up in writing with corrections or additional facts, and keep a copy.
  6. Talk to an employment lawyer if you're accused, suspended or retaliated against.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to cooperate with an HR investigation in Texas?

Generally yes. In an at-will job, a private employer can require employees to participate in workplace investigations and can discipline employees who refuse.

Can I bring a lawyer to an HR meeting?

Private employers usually do not have to allow an employee's attorney in an internal investigation interview, but you can consult a lawyer before and after the meeting.

What are Weingarten rights?

Union-represented employees can request a union representative during an investigatory interview they reasonably believe may lead to discipline. The employer must allow it, end the interview, or let the employee choose whether to continue without a representative.

Can I see my personnel file in Texas?

Texas law does not require private employers to give employees access to their personnel files, although many employers allow it. Employees can access OSHA injury and illness records, and public employees can use the Public Information Act.

Can I be retaliated against for reporting harassment during an investigation?

No. Retaliating against an employee for reporting harassment or discrimination, or for participating in an EEOC proceeding, is illegal under federal and Texas law.
