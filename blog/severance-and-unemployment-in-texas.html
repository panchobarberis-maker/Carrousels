Can You Get Severance and Unemployment in Texas?

Short answer

Usually yes, but not always at the same time. The Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) treats only two kinds of termination pay as affecting your unemployment: severance your employer promised in advance on its own, such as in a written policy or offer letter, and wages paid in lieu of notice. Those payments delay your benefits until the period they cover ends; they don't cancel them. Money paid in exchange for signing a release of claims, or under a negotiated agreement, generally doesn't affect your unemployment at all. You still have to report every payment, and you still must have lost your job for a reason other than misconduct.

You've been let go and handed a severance offer. The next question is almost always: "If I take the severance, can I still collect unemployment?" In Texas, the answer depends less on the dollar amount and more on why the money is being paid and why your job ended.

When a payment does count, TWC treats you as covered for the period the payment represents, and benefits begin after that period ends. TWC decides each claim on its own facts, so how the agreement and paycheck stubs describe the payment matters.

2Lump Sum vs. Salary Continuation

Many articles say a lump sum never delays benefits and salary continuation always does. That's an oversimplification. The reason for the payment is what counts under TWC's rules, but the form still matters in practice:

  • Salary continuation: if you stay on payroll for, say, eight more weeks, TWC will usually treat those weeks as paid, and benefits start after they end.
  • Lump sum promised by policy: TWC can assign it to the weeks it represents, which delays benefits for that period.
  • Lump sum for a release: generally doesn't affect your benefits, but you still report it.

3Laid Off vs. Fired: Why It Matters

  • Laid off because of downsizing, budget cuts, a closure or position elimination: you're generally eligible. See layoffs when a company closes.
  • Fired: you can still qualify unless TWC finds you were fired for misconduct connected with the work, and the employer generally has to prove it. Read can you collect unemployment if you were fired.
  • Quit or "offered to resign": you need good cause connected with the work. Being pushed to resign in exchange for severance can still be treated as a discharge, so don't let the paperwork call it a voluntary quit if it wasn't.

4What to Check in the Severance Agreement

  • How the payment is labeled: severance, pay in lieu of notice, or consideration for a release
  • Whether the separation is described as a layoff, termination or resignation
  • Any promise that the employer won't contest your unemployment claim
  • The release of claims: what legal rights you give up, and the deadline to sign
  • Confidentiality and non-disparagement terms

Signing a severance agreement doesn't by itself take away your right to unemployment. But you may be giving up claims worth more than the payment. If you think you were let go because of discrimination, retaliation or a complaint, get advice before signing. See when to talk to a severance lawyer.

5If Your Employer Contests Your Claim

Employers sometimes contest claims to protect their tax rate. A challenge isn't a loss. TWC looks at why the job ended, whether there was misconduct, and how the severance was paid. If you're denied, you have 14 calendar days from the mailing date to appeal, and you should keep requesting payment while the appeal is pending.

✅What to Do After a Layoff With Severance

  1. Apply for unemployment right away; don't wait for the severance to run out.
  2. Report every payment accurately: severance, pay in lieu of notice and final wages.
  3. Keep the agreement and pay stubs that show how and why the money was paid.
  4. Read the release before signing and note the deadline.
  5. Appeal within 14 days if TWC denies or delays your claim and you disagree.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get severance and unemployment at the same time in Texas?

Sometimes. Severance the employer promised in advance by policy and wages in lieu of notice delay unemployment until the period they cover ends. Payments made in exchange for signing a release of claims generally do not affect unemployment benefits.

Does a lump-sum severance delay unemployment in Texas?

It depends on why it was paid. A lump sum promised in advance by company policy can delay benefits for the period it represents, while a lump sum paid in exchange for a release of claims generally does not.

Does signing a severance agreement disqualify me from unemployment?

No. Signing a severance agreement does not by itself disqualify you. Eligibility still depends on why your job ended and how the payment is classified.

Do I have to report severance pay to TWC?

Yes. Report severance and wages in lieu of notice when you apply and when TWC asks. Failing to report payments can lead to overpayments and penalties.

Can I get unemployment if I was fired and got severance?

Often, yes. A firing disqualifies you only if it was for misconduct connected with the work, which the employer generally must prove.
