Employment Background Checks in Texas: What's Legal and Your Rights

Short answer

Yes, Texas employers can legally run background checks on applicants and employees. If they use a background check company, the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) requires a separate written disclosure and your written permission first, and if they plan to reject or fire you because of the report, they must give you a copy of it and a summary of your rights before acting, then a final adverse action notice. Texas law generally bars background check companies from reporting arrests, indictments or convictions more than seven years old for jobs paying under $75,000 a year. Employers also can't use criminal records in a way that discriminates by race or national origin.

You got the offer, signed the authorization, and then the offer disappeared. Background checks are routine in Houston hiring, but they come with federal and Texas rules employers must follow, and a report can be wrong.

1The FCRA Steps Employers Must Follow

  1. Disclosure: a clear written notice, in a document by itself, that a background report may be obtained.
  2. Authorization: your written permission before the check is run.
  3. Pre-adverse action notice: before rejecting or firing you based on the report, a copy of the report and "A Summary of Your Rights Under the FCRA."
  4. Adverse action notice: after the decision, notice that it was based on the report, the background company's contact information, and your right to dispute it.

These rules apply when the employer uses an outside background check company. An employer that searches public records itself isn't covered by the FCRA, but anti-discrimination laws still apply.

2What Can Show Up, and How Far Back

Criminal recordsUnder Texas law, generally limited to seven years for jobs paying under $75,000 a year.
Employment and educationPast employers and schools can be verified. See employer references in Texas.
Credit reportsAllowed with your consent; some negative items age off after seven years.
Driving records and drug testsCommon for driving and safety-sensitive jobs. See drug testing at work in Texas.

Expunged records shouldn't appear on a report. Public social media can be viewed, but an employer can't use what it learns about your race, religion, pregnancy, disability or other protected traits to make the decision.

3Criminal Records and Discrimination

Texas has no statewide "ban the box" law for private employers, so they can ask about criminal history on applications. But the EEOC warns that blanket policies rejecting anyone with an arrest or conviction can discriminate by race or national origin. Employers should consider the nature of the offense, how long ago it happened, and whether it relates to the job. An arrest alone is not proof of wrongdoing.

4If the Report Is Wrong

  • Tell the employer right away, after the pre-adverse notice, that the report contains errors
  • Dispute it with the background check company; it generally must reinvestigate within 30 days
  • Keep copies of the report, the notices and your dispute
  • Common errors: someone else's record with a similar name, expunged or sealed cases, outdated charges, wrong dispositions

Background check companies and employers that violate the FCRA can owe damages and attorney's fees.

✅If a Background Check Cost You the Job

  1. Ask for a copy of the report and the notices if you didn't receive them.
  2. Check every entry for errors and records older than the legal limit.
  3. Dispute errors in writing with the background check company.
  4. Note whether others with similar records were hired.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer if the employer skipped the FCRA steps or the decision looks discriminatory.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal for employers to run background checks in Texas?

Yes. Employers can run background checks, but if they use a background check company they must give a separate written disclosure and get written permission under the FCRA.

How far back does a background check go in Texas?

Texas law generally prohibits background check companies from reporting arrests, indictments or convictions more than seven years old for jobs paying under $75,000 a year. Higher-paying jobs are not subject to that limit.

Can an employer reject me because of my criminal record in Texas?

Often yes, but blanket policies that exclude everyone with a record can be discriminatory, and the employer must follow the FCRA notice steps if it relies on a background report.

What if my background check has a mistake?

Tell the employer and dispute the error in writing with the background check company, which generally must reinvestigate within 30 days.

Do employers have to show me my background check?

If they plan to take adverse action based on a report from a background check company, they must give you a copy of the report and a summary of your FCRA rights before making the decision final.
