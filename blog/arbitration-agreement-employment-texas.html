Signed an Arbitration Agreement at Work? What It Means for Your Claims in Texas

Short answer

Many Texas employers require employees to sign arbitration agreements, and courts generally enforce them under the Federal Arbitration Act. That usually means employment claims, such as discrimination, wage or wrongful termination claims, go to a private arbitrator instead of a judge or jury, and class action waivers in those agreements are generally enforceable. But there are important limits: since March 3, 2022, a federal law lets employees choose to take sexual harassment and sexual assault claims to court even if they signed an arbitration agreement, and you can generally still file a charge with the EEOC or the Texas Workforce Commission. Whether your agreement is enforceable depends on its exact terms.

Arbitration clauses are often buried in onboarding paperwork, offer letters, handbooks or an "acknowledgment" you clicked on your first day. Most employees don't realize they signed one until they have a problem. Here's what an arbitration agreement does and doesn't take away from Houston employees.

👉 An arbitration agreement changes where your claim is decided. It doesn't erase your rights.

1What Arbitration Means

Private decision-makerAn arbitrator, not a judge or jury, decides the case.
Limited appealArbitration awards are very hard to overturn.
ConfidentialProceedings are usually private, unlike a public lawsuit.
Class waiversMany agreements require you to bring claims individually, not as a group.

Your substantive rights, such as protection from discrimination, retaliation and unpaid wages, generally still apply in arbitration.

2Sexual Harassment and Assault Claims Are Different

The Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act, signed March 3, 2022, lets the person bringing a sexual harassment or sexual assault claim choose to void a pre-dispute arbitration agreement or class waiver and go to court. It applies to disputes that arise or accrue on or after that date, and a court, not an arbitrator, decides whether the law applies.

The law doesn't cover other types of claims, like race or age discrimination. Learn more about sexual harassment claims in Texas and harassment at work.

3You Can Still Go to the EEOC or TWC

An arbitration agreement generally doesn't stop you from filing a charge of discrimination with the EEOC or the Texas Workforce Commission, or a wage claim with the TWC. The deadlines still apply: generally 180 days with the TWC or 300 days with the EEOC for discrimination. See filing an EEOC claim.

4What to Check in Your Agreement

  • Did you actually agree? Look for a signature, click-through or continued employment after notice.
  • Which claims does it cover? Some exclude wage claims or workers' comp.
  • Who pays? Employer-required arbitration usually puts most costs on the employer.
  • Can the employer change it alone? One-sided modification terms can make an agreement unenforceable in Texas.
  • Is there a class or collective action waiver?

Arbitration clauses often sit next to non-competes and confidentiality terms. See non-compete agreements in Texas and non-disparagement clauses.

✅Before You Sign, or Once You Have a Claim

  1. Ask for a copy of any arbitration agreement or handbook you're asked to accept.
  2. Read the scope and waiver sections carefully.
  3. Keep the signed version and the date you received it.
  4. Don't assume you have no options: agency charges and some claims may still be available.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer about whether the agreement is enforceable.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Are employment arbitration agreements enforceable in Texas?

Generally yes. Courts usually enforce arbitration agreements under the Federal Arbitration Act, although agreements can be challenged if there was no valid agreement, the employer can change the terms on its own, or the terms are unconscionable.

Can I sue my employer if I signed an arbitration agreement?

Usually your claims must go to arbitration instead of court. An important exception: sexual harassment and sexual assault claims arising on or after March 3, 2022 can be taken to court at the employee's choice.

Can I still file an EEOC charge if I signed an arbitration agreement?

Generally yes. An arbitration agreement does not prevent you from filing a charge of discrimination with the EEOC or the Texas Workforce Commission.

What is a class action waiver in an employment agreement?

It is a clause requiring you to bring claims individually rather than as part of a group lawsuit. Courts generally enforce these waivers in employment arbitration agreements, except where the 2022 sexual harassment law applies.

Do I have to sign an arbitration agreement to get a job?

An employer can generally require one as a condition of employment in Texas. Read it carefully and keep a copy.
