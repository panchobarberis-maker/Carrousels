Holiday Pay in Texas: Do You Get Paid Extra for Working Thanksgiving or Christmas?

Short answer

No law requires private employers in Texas to give paid holidays or to pay extra, such as time-and-a-half or double time, for working on Thanksgiving, Christmas or any other holiday. Holiday pay is a benefit set by your employer's policy or contract. Hours worked on a holiday are ordinary hours, and overtime is owed only when a non-exempt employee works more than 40 hours in the workweek. But if your employer promised holiday pay in a written policy or agreement, it can be recovered as wages under the Texas Payday Law, and an employer must consider a request for time off for a religious holiday under Title VII.

Retail, hospitals, refineries, restaurants and warehouses across Houston run straight through Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's. 1Is Holiday Pay Required in Texas?

The U.S. Department of Labor states that the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) does not require payment for time not worked, such as holidays, and does not require extra pay or double time for working on holidays, weekends or nights. Texas has no state law that adds a holiday pay requirement for private employers.

2Overtime During Holiday Weeks

For non-exempt (usually hourly) employees, overtime at time-and-a-half is owed for hours actually worked over 40 in the workweek. Two rules trip people up:

  • Working on the holiday itself doesn't trigger overtime. A holiday shift counts like any other day toward the 40 hours.
  • A paid holiday you didn't work generally doesn't count toward the 40 hours for overtime purposes, unless your employer's policy says it does.

If you worked more than 40 hours during a busy holiday week and didn't get time-and-a-half, see our guide to overtime in Texas.

3Can Your Employer Make You Work on a Holiday?

Generally, yes. In an at-will state, an employer can schedule you on Thanksgiving or Christmas and discipline you for not showing up. Read more about when your boss can change your schedule and denied vacation requests. Important exceptions:

  • Religious holidays: under Title VII, employers with 15 or more employees must accommodate a sincere religious observance, like Christmas, Hanukkah or Eid, unless it would cause undue hardship, which the Supreme Court has said means substantial increased costs.
  • Uneven treatment: holiday shifts that consistently land on employees of one race, sex, religion or age can be discrimination.
  • Retaliation: being stuck with every holiday after complaining about pay or harassment can be illegal retaliation.

See religion in the workplace for more on religious accommodations.

4When Promised Holiday Pay Isn't Paid

Under the Texas Payday Law, holiday pay can be claimed as wages only if a written agreement or written policy provides for it. If your handbook promises double time for Christmas or a paid day off for Thanksgiving and you didn't get it:

  1. Save the handbook or policy that promises holiday pay.
  2. Compare it to your pay stub for the holiday week.
  3. Ask HR in writing for the missing pay.
  4. File a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when the pay was due, or talk to a lawyer about unpaid wages.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is holiday pay required by law in Texas?

No. Neither federal law nor Texas law requires private employers to give paid holidays or to pay extra for working on a holiday. Holiday pay is a benefit set by the employer's policy or a contract.

Do I get time-and-a-half for working on Thanksgiving or Christmas?

Only if your employer's policy or contract provides it. Under federal law, holiday hours are ordinary hours, and overtime is owed only for hours worked over 40 in a workweek.

Does a paid holiday count toward overtime?

Generally no. Paid time off that is not worked, including holidays, does not count toward the 40 hours needed for overtime unless the employer's policy says otherwise.

Can I be fired for refusing to work on a holiday in Texas?

Usually yes, because Texas is an at-will state. However, employers with 15 or more employees must reasonably accommodate religious observance unless it causes undue hardship, and assigning holidays in a discriminatory or retaliatory way can be illegal.

What can I do if my employer didn't pay promised holiday pay?

If holiday pay was promised in a written policy or agreement, you can file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when it was due, or consult an employment lawyer.
