Seasonal and Temp Workers in Texas: Your Rights This Holiday Season

Short answer

Seasonal and temporary employees in Texas have the same core wage and anti-discrimination rights as permanent employees: at least the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour, overtime for non-exempt hours over 40 in a workweek, payment of final wages on time under the Texas Payday Law, and protection from discrimination, harassment and retaliation. If you're placed by a staffing agency, both the agency and the client company can share responsibility for wages and safety. Temp workers have special unemployment rules in Texas: you're not considered unemployed until three business days after an assignment ends, and if the agency told you in advance, you must contact it for a new assignment or you may be treated as having quit.

Every fall, Houston-area retailers, warehouses, delivery services and event venues hire thousands of seasonal and temp workers for the holiday rush. Short-term doesn't mean fewer rights. Here's what seasonal and agency workers are entitled to, and what to watch out for when the season ends.

1Wages and Overtime

  • Minimum wage: Texas follows the federal minimum of $7.25 an hour. See our minimum wage guide for Houston.
  • Overtime: non-exempt seasonal workers get time-and-a-half for hours over 40 in a workweek, even during peak season. Read whether your boss can force you to work overtime.
  • All hours worked count: required pre-shift meetings, work done before clocking in and tasks finished after clocking out are generally compensable.
  • Holiday pay: not required by law unless promised. See holiday pay in Texas.

2Staffing Agencies vs. the Client Company

Who pays youUsually the agency, which must pay on time and follow the Texas Payday Law.

SafetyOSHA treats the agency and the host employer as jointly responsible for temp workers' safety.

3Discrimination and Harassment Protections

Federal and Texas anti-discrimination laws protect temp and seasonal workers. It's illegal to end an assignment, refuse to hire or harass you because of race, sex, pregnancy, religion, age, disability or national origin, or because you complained. Agencies can't honor a client's discriminatory request, such as "send us younger workers."

  • Your assignment ended right after you reported harassment
  • You were told you weren't "a fit" after disclosing a pregnancy
  • Only workers of one race or age group were kept on after the season

Learn more about harassment at work.

4When the Season or Assignment Ends

  • Final pay: if you're let go, final wages are due within 6 calendar days. If you quit, by the next regular payday. See the Texas Payday Law.
  • Temp agency workers: you aren't considered unemployed until three business days after the assignment ends. If the agency told you about the rule, contact it for a new assignment, or you may be treated as having quit.
  • Seasonal workers: you can apply for unemployment when the job ends. Eligibility depends on your past wages and the reason the job ended.

✅Protect Yourself This Season

  1. Keep your own record of hours, including pre-shift meetings and work off the clock.
  2. Save pay stubs and compare them to your hours.
  3. Get your agency's reassignment rules in writing.
  4. Report harassment or safety problems in writing to both the agency and the client.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer if you're shorted on pay or let go after a complaint.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Do seasonal workers get overtime in Texas?

Yes. Non-exempt seasonal and temporary employees must be paid time-and-a-half for hours worked over 40 in a workweek, the same as permanent employees.

What is the minimum wage for temp workers in Texas?

Texas follows the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour, which applies to seasonal and temporary employees as well.

Who is responsible if a staffing agency doesn't pay me?

The agency is usually your employer for payroll, but the client company that directs your work can also share legal responsibility for wages and working conditions.

Can temp workers get unemployment in Texas?

Yes, if eligible. A temp worker is not considered unemployed until three business days after an assignment ends, and must contact the agency for reassignment if advised to, or may be treated as having quit.

Are temp workers protected from discrimination?

Yes. Anti-discrimination and anti-retaliation laws protect temporary and seasonal workers, and agencies cannot follow a client's discriminatory requests.
