Employer Overpaid You in Texas? Can They Take It Back From Your Paycheck?

Short answer

If your employer genuinely overpaid you, you'll usually have to give the money back, but in Texas the employer generally can't just take it out of your next paycheck on its own. Under the Texas Payday Law, a deduction from wages requires a court order, a law that authorizes it, or your written authorization for a lawful purpose, and the Texas Workforce Commission treats overpayments the same as wage advances and loans: the deduction must be authorized in writing. A signed handbook acknowledgment can count only if it specifically describes the deduction. Without valid authorization, the employer has to ask you to repay or pursue it another way. If an unauthorized deduction was taken, you can file a wage claim with TWC within 180 days.

A bigger deposit than usual, a duplicate payment, a raise applied twice. Payroll mistakes happen, and when the employer notices, the next paycheck can come up short without warning. Here's what you owe, what the employer can deduct, and how to push back when it does it the wrong way.

TWC's own sample policy treats an overpayment as an advance on future wages, repaid from upcoming paychecks, and recommends a separately signed authorization. For other deductions, see the Texas Payday Law.

2Overpayment vs. a Pay Cut in Disguise

  • The employer calls it an "overpayment," but it's really pay you earned, like overtime, commissions or a bonus it now regrets
  • The amount is wrong or unexplained, with no pay stubs or calculation
  • The employer is lowering your rate retroactively for hours already worked
  • The deduction shows up right after you complained about pay, safety or discrimination

An employer can change your pay going forward with notice, but not for work already done. See can my employer reduce my wages in Texas.

3Final Paychecks and Overpayments

A fired employee must get final pay within six calendar days, and someone who quits by the next regular payday. TWC has ruled against employers that took an entire overpayment out of a final check under an unwritten arrangement. If you're leaving, ask for any repayment terms in writing. See two weeks' notice in Texas and what to do in the first 48 hours after being fired.

4Federal Minimum Wage and Overtime

The U.S. Department of Labor generally treats a documented overpayment like a wage advance, so recovering it isn't automatically a federal minimum wage violation. But other deductions can't cut non-exempt pay below minimum wage or reduce overtime owed, and Texas's written-authorization rule still applies. Unemployment overpayments from TWC are a separate issue with their own process.

✅What to Do If Your Paycheck Was Cut

  1. Ask for the calculation in writing: dates, amounts and why it's considered an overpayment.
  2. Check your pay stubs and anything you signed about deductions.
  3. Offer a repayment plan if you agree you were overpaid, and get it in writing.
  4. Dispute it in writing if the amount is wrong or it's pay you earned.
  5. File a TWC wage claim within 180 days of when the wages were due, or talk to a lawyer. See unpaid wages attorney in Texas.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to pay back an overpayment from my employer in Texas?

Usually yes, if you were genuinely overpaid. But the employer generally needs your written authorization to take it out of your wages; otherwise it must ask you to repay or pursue it another way.

Can my employer deduct an overpayment from my paycheck without permission?

Generally no. Under the Texas Payday Law, the Texas Workforce Commission treats overpayment deductions like advances and loans, which must be authorized in writing by the employee.

Does a signed handbook count as authorization?

Only if the acknowledgment specifically informs you of the deduction, gives you a reasonable expectation of the amount, and states that you agree to be bound by it.

Can my employer take an overpayment out of my final paycheck?

Only with valid written authorization. TWC has ruled against employers that deducted overpayments from final pay based on unwritten arrangements.

How do I dispute an unauthorized paycheck deduction in Texas?

Ask for the calculation in writing, dispute it in writing, and file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when the wages were due.
