Can Your Boss Monitor You? Workplace Surveillance Laws in Texas

Short answer

Mostly, yes. Texas employers can generally monitor company email, computers, chat tools like Slack and Teams, and work phones, especially when a written policy tells employees they have no expectation of privacy. Video cameras without sound are generally allowed in work areas. But there are limits: Texas is a one-party consent state, so recording conversations no one involved agreed to is a crime; audio recording requires notice and consent; cameras can never be placed where people undress, such as restrooms; an employer must stop listening once a call is clearly personal; capturing fingerprints or face scans requires notice and consent; and spying on employees' protected union or group activity violates federal labor law.

Keystroke trackers, screenshots every ten minutes, GPS in the company truck, cameras over the register, "productivity scores" for remote workers. Workplace monitoring has exploded, and many Houston employees don't know what their employer can see, or what crosses the line.

👉 On company systems, assume you're being watched. But some types of surveillance are illegal, even at work.

1Email, Computers, Slack and Teams

An employer that owns the systems can generally monitor what happens on them. The Texas Workforce Commission's guide for employers recommends a written policy telling employees that company systems may be monitored and that they have no expectation of privacy, and Texas courts have sided with employers when such a policy exists. In McLaren v. Microsoft, a Dallas appeals court rejected an invasion-of-privacy claim over an employer's review of an employee's work email.

2Phone Calls and Recording Conversations

Texas is a one-party consent state: a conversation can be recorded if at least one person in it agrees. Recording a conversation when no party has consented is a felony.

  • Employers can generally monitor business calls on company phones, and should tell employees and callers that calls may be monitored.
  • Once a call is clearly personal, the employer may listen only long enough to determine that, and continuing to listen risks an invasion-of-privacy claim.
  • Because of one-party consent, you can generally record your own conversations with a manager in Texas, but check your employer's policies first, since violating a no-recording rule can still get you disciplined.

If you find a camera in a restroom, locker room or changing area at work, report it to police and HR immediately. Learn about harassment at work.

4Fingerprint and Face-Scan Time Clocks

Texas's Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act requires notice and consent before a business captures a fingerprint, retina or iris scan, voiceprint, or face or hand geometry for a commercial purpose. It also limits selling or sharing that data and generally requires it to be destroyed within a year after the purpose for collecting it ends. Only the Texas Attorney General can enforce the law, with civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation.

5When Monitoring Becomes Illegal Retaliation

Even lawful monitoring tools can be used illegally. Warning signs:

  • Your manager started tracking your messages or activity only after you complained about harassment, discrimination or unpaid wages
  • The company is watching employees' union or group discussions about pay and conditions, which can violate the National Labor Relations Act
  • Monitoring is applied to you but not to coworkers of a different race, sex or age
  • Information gathered is used to expose your medical condition

See retaliation in the workplace and getting fired for a social media post.

✅Protect Yourself

  1. Read your employer's technology and monitoring policy, and keep a copy.
  2. Keep personal matters off company devices and accounts.
  3. Don't use work email to talk to a lawyer about a claim against your employer.
  4. Write down when monitoring changed, especially after a complaint.
  5. Report cameras in private areas right away.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer read my work email in Texas?

Generally yes. Employers can monitor company email and messaging systems, especially when a written policy tells employees they have no expectation of privacy. Texas courts have rejected privacy claims over employers reviewing work email.

Can my boss record my phone calls?

Employers can generally monitor business calls on company phones and should give notice. Texas is a one-party consent state, and an employer must stop listening once it is clear a call is personal.

Is it legal to have cameras at work in Texas?

Video-only cameras are generally legal in work areas. Recording audio requires notice and consent, and cameras are never allowed in restrooms or changing areas, where invasive visual recording is a felony.

Can I record a conversation with my boss in Texas?

Texas is a one-party consent state, so you can generally record a conversation you take part in. However, your employer may still discipline you for violating a workplace no-recording policy.

Can my employer require a fingerprint time clock?

Texas law requires notice and consent before a business captures biometric identifiers like fingerprints or face scans, and limits how that data is shared and how long it is kept.
