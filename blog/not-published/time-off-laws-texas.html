Time Off Work in Texas: Which Leave Is Required by Law (and Which Isn't)

Short answer

Texas law requires very little paid time off for private employees. There's no state law requiring paid sick days, vacation, holidays or bereavement leave; those come from your employer's policy. What the law does protect: unpaid, job-protected FMLA leave for eligible employees (up to 12 weeks), time off to vote when your shift covers poll hours, jury service, military service under USERRA, reasonable break time to pump breast milk, and accommodations for pregnancy, disability and religious practice. Being fired for using protected leave can be illegal, even in an at-will state.

"Do they have to give me the day off?" is one of the most common questions Houston employees ask. The answer depends on why you need the time, how big your employer is, and what your handbook says. This guide puts every type of leave in one place, with links to our detailed article on each.

2Leave That's Up to Your Employer

  • Sick days: Texas has no statewide paid sick leave law. See paid sick leave in Texas.
  • Vacation: not required, and payout at the end of employment is owed only if a written policy says so. See whether your employer can deny vacation.
  • Holidays: no paid holidays or holiday premium required. See holiday pay in Texas.
  • Bereavement: no funeral leave required, though FMLA or other laws may apply. See bereavement leave in Texas.
  • Parental leave: no paid leave required; FMLA may cover bonding time. See maternity leave in Texas.
Your policy is enforceable

If your handbook promises paid leave and your employer applies it to some employees but not others, that inconsistency can be evidence of discrimination.

3When Taking Time Off Gets You Fired

Texas is at-will, so missing work without approved leave can lead to discipline. But firing you for using protected leave is illegal. Warning signs:

  • You were fired during or right after FMLA leave. See fired while on FMLA.
  • You were fired for calling in sick with a serious health condition. See fired for calling in sick.
  • You lost your job after jury service, voting or military duty
  • Your request for a pregnancy or disability accommodation was followed by a firing

✅Before You Request Time Off

  1. Read your handbook's leave policies and save a copy.
  2. Request leave in writing and say why, especially for medical, family, voting or jury reasons.
  3. Ask whether the leave is FMLA-protected if it's for a serious health condition.
  4. Keep documentation: jury summons, military orders, doctor's notes.
  5. Talk to a lawyer if you're disciplined or fired after asking.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is paid time off required in Texas?

No. Texas law does not require private employers to provide paid sick days, vacation, holidays or bereavement leave. Paid time off comes from the employer's policy or a contract.

What leave is protected by law in Texas?

Protected leave includes FMLA leave for eligible employees, time off to vote when your shift covers poll hours, jury service, military service under USERRA, break time to pump breast milk, and leave as a pregnancy, disability or religious accommodation.

Can I be fired for taking time off in Texas?

You can be disciplined for unapproved absences in an at-will state, but firing you for using protected leave, such as FMLA, jury duty, voting or military leave, is illegal.

Does my employer have to pay out unused vacation in Texas?

Only if a written policy or agreement provides for payout. Texas law does not otherwise require it.

Who is eligible for FMLA leave?

Employees who have worked for a covered employer for at least 12 months and 1,250 hours in the past year, at a location with 50 or more employees within 75 miles.
