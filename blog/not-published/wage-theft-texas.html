Wage Theft in Texas: 9 Ways Employers Underpay Workers (and How to Recover It)

Short answer

Wage theft is when an employer doesn't pay wages you've legally earned. In Texas, the most common forms are unpaid overtime, off-the-clock work, automatic meal deductions for breaks you didn't take, unpaid on-call or training time, misclassifying employees as exempt or as independent contractors, tip violations, unpaid promised bonuses or commissions, and late or short final paychecks. You can file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when the wages were due, or bring a claim under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, which generally has a two-year deadline (three years for willful violations) and can include an equal amount in liquidated damages.

Most wage theft doesn't look like theft. It looks like "clock out and finish up," a 30-minute lunch deduction you never get, a salary that's supposed to cover "whatever it takes," or a bonus that disappears when you give notice. A few minutes a day can add up to thousands of dollars a year. Here are the nine patterns we see most often in Houston, with links to our detailed guides.

1The 9 Most Common Forms of Wage Theft

  1. Unpaid overtime for non-exempt hours over 40 in a workweek. See our overtime guide and "unauthorized" overtime.
  2. Off-the-clock work: being told to clock out and keep working, or to start before you clock in.
  3. Skipped or interrupted meal breaks that are still deducted. See lunch and rest breaks in Texas.
  4. Unpaid on-call, callback and training time. See on-call and training pay.
  5. Misclassification as "exempt": a salary alone doesn't exempt you from overtime. See the overtime exemption rule.
  6. Misclassification as an independent contractor. See how employees are classified.
  7. Tip violations: illegal tip pools or managers keeping tips. See our tipping guide.
  8. Unpaid bonuses and commissions that were promised under set terms. See withheld bonuses and commission pay.
  9. Late or short final pay: due within 6 days if you're fired, or by the next payday if you quit. See the Texas Payday Law.

2Two Ways to Recover Unpaid Wages

Texas Payday Law claim (TWC)File with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when the wages were due. Good for unpaid wages, final pay and promised benefits in a written policy.
FLSA claim (federal)For minimum wage and overtime. Generally 2 years to file, 3 for willful violations, with possible liquidated (double) damages and attorney's fees.

Which path is better depends on the amount, the type of pay and your deadlines. See our guide to unpaid wages in Texas.

3Retaliation for Complaining About Pay Is Illegal

It's illegal to fire, demote or cut the hours of an employee for complaining about unpaid wages or overtime, or for filing a wage claim. Learn more about wage retaliation.

✅How to Build Your Wage Claim

  1. Keep your own record of hours: start, stop, breaks and calls.
  2. Save pay stubs and compare them to your hours.
  3. Keep written policies: handbook, bonus plan, commission agreement.
  4. Save texts and emails asking you to work off the clock.
  5. Act within the deadlines: 180 days for TWC, generally 2 years for the FLSA.

?Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as wage theft in Texas?

Wage theft includes unpaid overtime, off-the-clock work, deducting breaks you didn't take, unpaid on-call or training time, misclassification, tip violations, unpaid promised bonuses or commissions, and late or short final paychecks.

How do I report wage theft in Texas?

You can file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission under the Texas Payday Law within 180 days of when the wages were due, or bring a claim under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act for minimum wage and overtime violations.

How far back can I recover unpaid overtime?

Under the FLSA, generally two years, or three years if the violation was willful. Texas Payday Law claims must be filed within 180 days of when the wages were due.

Can I get double damages for unpaid wages?

Under the FLSA, employees may recover an equal amount as liquidated damages in addition to the unpaid minimum wage or overtime, plus attorney's fees, in many cases.

Can I be fired for complaining about unpaid wages?

No. Retaliating against an employee for complaining about wages or filing a wage claim is illegal.
