Fired After a Work Injury in Texas? Workers' Comp Retaliation and Your Rights

Short answer

If your employer carries workers' compensation insurance, Texas Labor Code Section 451.001 makes it illegal to fire or otherwise discriminate against you because you filed a workers' comp claim in good faith, hired a lawyer for the claim, started a proceeding or testified in one. These claims go straight to court without an agency charge, and Texas courts generally apply a two-year deadline. Texas is unusual because private employers can opt out of workers' comp; the Texas Supreme Court has held that Chapter 451 does not apply to these "nonsubscribers," but injured workers can sue them for negligence. Firing an injured worker can also violate the ADA or the FMLA.

Refineries, plants, construction sites, warehouses and hospitals: Houston has some of the most physically demanding jobs in the country. When someone gets hurt, the next fear is often losing the job on top of the injury. Texas law protects workers who use the workers' comp system, but the rules depend on one question many employees can't answer: does my employer carry workers' comp?

1What Texas Law Prohibits

Under Section 451.001 of the Texas Labor Code, an employer may not discharge or in any other manner discriminate against an employee because the employee:

  • Filed a workers' compensation claim in good faith
  • Hired a lawyer to represent them in the claim
  • Started a proceeding under the Texas Workers' Compensation Act in good faith
  • Testified or is about to testify in a proceeding

"Discriminate" covers more than firing, including demotions, pay cuts, schedule changes and harassment tied to the claim. If you win, remedies can include lost wages, reinstatement and other damages.

2Subscribers vs. Nonsubscribers

Employer has workers' compChapter 451 applies. Retaliation for filing a claim is illegal, and you generally can't sue the employer for the injury itself.
Nonsubscriber employerChapter 451 does not apply (Texas Mexican Railway v. Bouchet, 1998). But you can sue for negligence, and the employer loses key defenses like blaming coworkers or your own negligence.
How to find outEmployers must notify employees whether they carry coverage. Ask HR, check posters at work or contact the Texas Department of Insurance.
Either wayFederal laws like the ADA and FMLA still protect you.

3Other Laws That Protect Injured Workers

  • ADA: if your injury limits a major life activity, you may be entitled to reasonable accommodations like light duty, a modified schedule or leave, and can't be fired because of the disability. See our disability discrimination guide.
  • FMLA: eligible employees can take up to 12 weeks of job-protected leave for a serious health condition. Read whether you have to use FMLA while on workers' comp.
  • OSHA: reporting a work injury or unsafe condition is protected activity.
Neutral policies can still matter

Texas courts have allowed employers to enforce a uniformly applied absence-control policy, such as termination after a set period of leave. But if the policy is applied only to injured workers, or used as a cover for retaliation, that can be illegal.

4Warning Signs of Retaliation

  • You were fired shortly after reporting the injury or filing a claim
  • Your manager told you not to file, or said it would "cause problems"
  • Sudden write-ups for issues that were never raised before
  • Your hours, duties or pay were cut after you hired a lawyer for your claim
  • Other employees broke the same rule but weren't fired

✅What to Do After a Work Injury

  1. Report the injury to your employer in writing, generally within 30 days, and keep a copy.
  2. Get medical treatment and follow your doctor's restrictions.
  3. Find out whether your employer carries workers' comp.
  4. Save texts, emails and write-ups before and after the injury.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer before signing any resignation or severance paperwork.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can I be fired for filing a workers' comp claim in Texas?

No, if your employer carries workers' compensation. Texas Labor Code Section 451.001 prohibits firing or discriminating against an employee for filing a good-faith claim, hiring a lawyer, starting a proceeding or testifying.

What if my employer doesn't have workers' comp?

Texas lets private employers opt out. The Texas Supreme Court has held that the Chapter 451 retaliation law does not apply to nonsubscribers, but injured employees can sue them for negligence, and the ADA and FMLA may still protect against firing.

How long do I have to file a workers' comp retaliation lawsuit in Texas?

Chapter 451 claims are filed directly in court without an agency charge, and Texas courts generally apply a two-year limitations period from the firing. Talk to a lawyer promptly to protect your deadline.

Can I be fired while on workers' comp?

You cannot be fired because of the claim. An employer may enforce a neutral, uniformly applied policy, but applying it only to injured workers or using it as an excuse can be illegal retaliation or disability discrimination.

Is a work injury a disability under the ADA?

It can be. If the injury substantially limits a major life activity, the ADA may require reasonable accommodations, such as light duty or leave, and prohibits firing you because of the disability.
