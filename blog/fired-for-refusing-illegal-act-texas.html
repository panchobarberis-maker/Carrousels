Fired for Refusing to Break the Law in Texas? The Sabine Pilot Exception Explained

Short answer

Yes, it's illegal in Texas to fire an employee for the sole reason that they refused to commit an illegal act that carries criminal penalties. The Texas Supreme Court created this narrow exception to at-will employment in Sabine Pilot Service, Inc. v. Hauck (1985). To win, you must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that your refusal was the only reason you were fired. The claim is a tort, so you may recover lost wages, other damages and, with proper proof, punitive damages. It protects refusing to break the law, not reporting someone else's violations, which is covered by separate whistleblower laws.

"Falsify the logbook." "Dump it in the drain." "Sign off on the inspection anyway." Houston employees in energy, trucking, healthcare, construction and finance are sometimes told to do something they know is illegal. When they say no, some get fired. Texas is an at-will state, but it does not let employers force you to choose between your job and committing a crime.

👉 You can't be fired just for refusing to commit a crime for your employer. But the rule is narrow, so the facts matter.

1The Case That Created the Rule

In Sabine Pilot, a deckhand was told to pump a boat's bilge water into the water. He saw a posted notice that it was illegal, confirmed it with the U.S. Coast Guard, refused, and was fired. The Texas Supreme Court held that public policy, as expressed in criminal laws, requires a very narrow exception to at-will employment: an employer can't fire an employee for the sole reason that the employee refused to perform an illegal act.

The court later confirmed in Safeshred, Inc. v. Martinez (2012) that the claim sounds in tort and can support punitive damages with proper proof.

2What You Have to Prove

  • Your employer asked or required you to do something illegal
  • The act would have exposed you to criminal penalties
  • You refused
  • Your refusal was the sole reason you were fired
The "sole reason" problem

Employers often point to another reason, such as performance, attendance or a policy violation. Strong reviews, close timing and inconsistent explanations can help show that the refusal was the only real reason. See when "cause" is a cover for an illegal firing.

3Examples That May Qualify

Falsifying recordsAltering safety logs, driver hours, inspection reports or medical records.
Environmental violationsIllegally dumping chemicals or waste, as in Sabine Pilot itself.
FraudBilling for services not provided, or misleading customers or the government.
Unsafe, illegal operationDriving over legal hour limits or operating without a required license.

4Refusing vs. Reporting: Whistleblower Laws

Sabine Pilot protects refusing to break the law. It doesn't cover being fired for reporting someone else's illegal conduct. Texas has no general whistleblower law for private employees (the Texas Whistleblower Act covers public employees), but federal laws protect many reports, including workplace safety complaints, securities fraud and certain financial and environmental violations. Read more about whistleblower protections in Texas and retaliation in the workplace.

✅If You're Asked to Do Something Illegal

  1. Don't do it. Calmly say you believe it's illegal and you won't do it.
  2. Put it in writing: a short email confirming what you were asked and that you declined.
  3. Check the law when you can, as the deckhand in Sabine Pilot did with the Coast Guard.
  4. Save your performance reviews and any write-ups before and after.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer quickly. These claims generally must be filed within two years of the firing.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can I be fired for refusing to do something illegal in Texas?

No. Under Sabine Pilot Service, Inc. v. Hauck, a Texas employer cannot fire an employee for the sole reason that the employee refused to perform an illegal act that carries criminal penalties.

What is the Sabine Pilot exception?

It is a narrow exception to at-will employment created by the Texas Supreme Court in 1985. It protects employees fired solely because they refused to commit an illegal act.

What do I have to prove in a Sabine Pilot claim?

You must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that you were asked to commit an act carrying criminal penalties, you refused, and the refusal was the sole reason you were fired.

Can I get punitive damages for being fired for refusing an illegal act?

Possibly. The Texas Supreme Court held in Safeshred, Inc. v. Martinez that a Sabine Pilot claim sounds in tort and allows punitive damages upon proper proof.

Does Sabine Pilot protect me if I report illegal activity?

No. It protects refusing to commit an illegal act, not reporting one. Reports may be protected by federal whistleblower laws or, for public employees, the Texas Whistleblower Act.
