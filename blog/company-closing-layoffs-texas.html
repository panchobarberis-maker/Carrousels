Laid Off Because Your Company Is Closing or Going Bankrupt in Texas? What Happens to Your Pay

Short answer

Even if your employer is shutting down, Texas law still requires final pay within six calendar days of a layoff, and you can file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days if you aren't paid. If the company files for bankruptcy, unpaid wages, salaries, commissions and certain vacation, severance and sick pay earned within 180 days before the filing or closing get priority up to $17,150 per employee for cases filed on or after April 1, 2025. Larger employers may also owe 60 days' notice or pay under the WARN Act. Your 401(k) money is held in a separate trust, not owned by the company. Health coverage may end if the company ends its plan, so act quickly on your options.

When a company closes its doors, employees are often the last to know: a sudden all-hands meeting, a locked building, a final paycheck that bounces. Houston has seen closures across oil services, restaurants, retail chains, healthcare groups and startups. Here's how to protect the money you've already earned.

1Final Pay and Unpaid Wages

Under the Texas Payday Law, an employee who is laid off must receive final pay within six calendar days. If you're not paid, file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days of when the wages were due. Unused vacation is owed only if a written policy provides for it. See our guide to unpaid wages in Texas.

2If Your Employer Files for Bankruptcy

Wage priorityWages, salaries and commissions, including certain vacation, severance and sick pay, earned within 180 days before the filing or closing.
The capUp to $17,150 per employee for cases filed on or after April 1, 2025. Amounts above the cap are general claims.
Benefit contributionsUnpaid contributions to employee benefit plans have a separate, similar priority.
File a proof of claimWatch for the bankruptcy court notice and file your claim by the deadline.

Priority claims are paid before most other unsecured debts, but payment isn't guaranteed if the company has little left.

3WARN Act Notice When a Company Closes

Employers with 100 or more employees generally must give 60 days' written notice before a plant closing or mass layoff. Without notice, employees may be owed back pay and benefits for the days of notice they didn't get. Exceptions exist for unforeseeable business circumstances and some "faltering company" situations, but employers must still give as much notice as practicable. Texas has no separate state WARN law. See the WARN Act in Texas.

4Your 401(k), Health Insurance and Severance

  • 401(k): retirement plan assets must be held in a trust separate from the company's money, so creditors generally can't take them. Contact the plan administrator about your options.
  • Health insurance: if the company keeps a group plan, you may be able to continue coverage under COBRA. If it ends all its plans, COBRA may not be available, so look at Marketplace coverage right away.
  • Severance: promised severance may become a claim in bankruptcy. Before signing a release, read what a typical severance package includes.
  • Retirement disputes: see our ERISA and retirement benefits guide.

✅What to Do If Your Company Is Closing

  1. Download pay stubs, W-2s and benefit statements before your access is cut off (only what you're permitted to keep).
  2. Write down unpaid hours, commissions and expenses.
  3. Apply for unemployment with the TWC right away. See severance and unemployment in Texas.
  4. File a TWC wage claim within 180 days if final pay doesn't arrive.
  5. Watch for bankruptcy notices and file a proof of claim on time.
  6. Talk to an employment lawyer about WARN Act and wage claims.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Do I still get my final paycheck if my company closes in Texas?

Yes. The Texas Payday Law requires final pay within six calendar days of a layoff, and you can file a wage claim with the Texas Workforce Commission within 180 days if it isn't paid.

What happens to unpaid wages if my employer files for bankruptcy?

Wages, salaries, commissions and certain vacation, severance and sick pay earned within 180 days before the filing or closing are priority claims up to $17,150 per employee for cases filed on or after April 1, 2025. Amounts above that are general unsecured claims.

Is my 401(k) safe if my company goes bankrupt?

Generally yes. Retirement plan assets must be held in a trust separate from the employer's assets, so the company's creditors generally cannot reach them.

Does a company have to give notice before closing?

Employers with 100 or more employees generally must give 60 days' notice under the WARN Act before a plant closing or mass layoff, subject to limited exceptions.

Can I get unemployment if my company shut down?

Yes. A layoff because the business closed is not a disqualifying reason, so you should apply with the Texas Workforce Commission right away.
