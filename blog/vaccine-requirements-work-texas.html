Can Your Employer Require a Flu Shot or Vaccine in Texas?

Short answer

It depends on the vaccine. Since February 6, 2024, Texas law bars private employers from requiring employees, contractors or applicants to get a COVID-19 vaccine, or taking adverse action against them for refusing, with a $50,000 penalty per violation enforced by the Texas Workforce Commission. For other vaccines, such as the flu shot, a Texas employer can generally require vaccination, but federal law still requires it to consider exemptions for employees with a disability or medical condition that prevents vaccination, and for sincerely held religious beliefs, unless an accommodation would cause undue hardship. Texas health care facilities must have a vaccine policy that includes medical exemptions.

Flu season brings a familiar email: "All employees must get vaccinated by December 1." Many Houston workers wonder whether they can say no, and what happens if they do. The answer is different for COVID-19 vaccines, for other vaccines like the flu shot, and for health care workers.

2Medical and Disability Exemptions

Under the ADA, an employer with 15 or more employees must consider a reasonable accommodation for an employee whose disability or medical condition prevents vaccination, such as a severe allergy. Possible accommodations include masking, testing, remote work or a schedule change, unless it would cause undue hardship or the employee would pose a direct threat that can't be reduced. Pregnancy-related limitations can also be accommodated under the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act. See reasonable accommodation in Texas.

3Religious Exemptions

Title VII requires employers with 15 or more employees to accommodate a sincerely held religious belief that conflicts with a vaccine requirement, unless it would cause undue hardship. Since the Supreme Court's 2023 decision in Groff v. DeJoy, undue hardship means a substantial burden in the context of the employer's business, not just a minor cost. The employer may ask questions about the belief if it has an objective reason to doubt it. See religion in the workplace.

4Warning Signs of an Illegal Response

  • You're fired or not hired for declining a COVID-19 vaccine at a private employer
  • Your exemption request is ignored or denied without any discussion
  • You're punished for asking for an accommodation
  • Other employees with similar requests got different treatment

Asking for an accommodation is protected; punishment for it can be retaliation. If you're out sick during flu season, see can you be fired for calling in sick in Texas.

✅How to Respond to a Vaccine Requirement

  1. Read the policy: which vaccine, the deadline, and how to request an exemption.
  2. Request an exemption in writing before the deadline, explaining the medical or religious reason.
  3. Provide a doctor's note for a medical exemption describing the limitation.
  4. Offer alternatives, such as masking or testing, and keep copies of every response.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer if you're disciplined, denied without discussion or fired. COVID-19 mandate complaints go to the TWC; discrimination charges generally to the EEOC within 300 days.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Can my employer require a COVID-19 vaccine in Texas?

No, if it is a private employer. Since February 6, 2024, Texas law prohibits private employers from requiring COVID-19 vaccination for employees, contractors and applicants, or taking adverse action against those who refuse.

Can my employer require a flu shot in Texas?

Generally yes, but the employer must consider exemptions for employees whose disability or medical condition prevents vaccination and for sincerely held religious beliefs, unless accommodation would cause undue hardship.

Can I be fired for refusing a flu shot?

Possibly, if you have no medical or religious basis for an exemption. If you requested an exemption based on a disability or religion, firing you without considering an accommodation may be illegal.

Do health care workers in Texas have to be vaccinated?

Texas health care facilities must have a vaccine-preventable disease policy that includes exemptions for medical contraindications, and may allow conscience or religious exemptions. Exempt workers may have to use protective equipment such as masks.

Where do I report a COVID-19 vaccine mandate in Texas?

Complaints about private employers requiring the COVID-19 vaccine can be filed with the Texas Workforce Commission, which can impose a $50,000 penalty per violation.
