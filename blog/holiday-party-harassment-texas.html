Harassed at the Office Holiday Party in Texas? Your Rights

Short answer

Yes, harassment at a company holiday party can be workplace harassment, even off-site and after hours. When the employer sponsors or organizes the event, sexual, racial or other harassment by a supervisor, coworker or even a client can make the employer liable, especially if it knew or should have known and didn't act. Alcohol is not an excuse. Texas sexual harassment law covers employers with one or more employees, and you generally have 300 days to file with the Texas Workforce Commission or the EEOC. Report what happened in writing, save messages and photos, and know that retaliating against you for reporting is illegal. If attendance is mandatory, non-exempt employees should generally be paid for the time.

An open bar, a supervisor who goes too far, a comment that won't stop, an "after-party" that isn't optional for the junior staff. Holiday season is when many Houston employees experience harassment they're unsure how to report, because it didn't happen "at work." Under the law, it often did.

1When a Party Counts as the Workplace

Company-sponsored partyPlanned, paid for or promoted by the employer, on-site or at a restaurant or venue.
Client and team eventsClient dinners, team outings and holiday happy hours tied to the job.
After-parties and messagesConduct that continues at an after-party, in a rideshare, or by text that night can still be part of a work harassment claim.
Who can harassSupervisors, coworkers, clients and vendors, if the employer knew or should have known and failed to act.

2Holiday Party Situations We Hear About

  • A manager keeps refilling your drink and insists you stay "for one more"
  • A slow dance, hug or kiss you didn't want, in front of the team
  • A Secret Santa gift or "joke" award that's sexual or mocks your race, religion or accent
  • An invitation to the after-party or hotel bar that comes with hints about your review
  • Photos or videos from the night posted in the team chat without consent
  • A client or vendor guest who crosses the line while your boss laughs it off

Whether it's legally harassment depends on how serious or repeated it is and whether it's tied to sex, race or another protected trait; see hostile work environment in Texas. A boss tying your job to saying yes is quid pro quo harassment. A physical assault can also be reported to the police.

3Alcohol, Religion and Mandatory Parties

  • Alcohol: being drunk doesn't excuse the harasser, and an employer that serves alcohol still has to respond to complaints.
  • Religion: employees can't be forced to take part in religious celebrations against their beliefs. See religion in the workplace.
  • Pay: if the party is truly voluntary and outside working hours, it usually isn't paid time. If attendance is required, non-exempt employees should generally be paid, including overtime if it pushes them past 40 hours.

4Your Own Conduct at the Party

The same rules work in reverse: Texas is an at-will state, and employees can be disciplined or fired for their own behavior at a company event, such as fights, drunk driving after the party or offensive posts about coworkers. See fired for a social media post in Texas. But an employee who reports harassment from the party can't be punished for reporting it; that's retaliation. For the follow-up process, see your rights in an HR investigation.

✅What to Do After the Party

  1. Write down what happened the same night or next morning: who, what, where, and who saw it.
  2. Save texts, DMs, photos and rideshare receipts; don't delete messages from the harasser.
  3. Report in writing to HR or the person your policy names, and keep a copy.
  4. Watch for changes in your schedule, assignments or reviews afterward.
  5. Talk to an employment lawyer; deadlines are generally 300 days with the EEOC or the TWC for sexual harassment. See filing an EEOC claim.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is harassment at a company holiday party considered workplace harassment?

It can be. Company-sponsored parties and events are generally treated as part of the work environment, even if they are off-site or after hours.

Is an after-party part of the work event?

It can be, especially if managers organize it, invite the team or attend. Harassment by coworkers or supervisors at an after-party can still be part of a workplace harassment claim.

Does it matter that everyone was drinking?

No. Alcohol does not excuse harassment, and an employer must still respond to complaints about conduct at events where alcohol was served.

Do I have to be paid for a mandatory holiday party?

Generally, if a non-exempt employee is required to attend, the time counts as hours worked. Truly voluntary social events outside working hours usually do not.

Can I skip the company holiday party?

Usually yes if it is voluntary. If attendance is required, non-exempt employees should generally be paid, and an employee can ask to be excused from religious parts of a celebration that conflict with their beliefs.
