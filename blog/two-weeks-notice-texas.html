Two Weeks' Notice in Texas: Do You Have to Give It, and Can You Be Let Go Early?

Short answer

No Texas law requires you to give two weeks' notice. In an at-will job you can quit at any time, unless a contract requires notice. Your employer can also accept your resignation immediately and generally doesn't have to pay you for the unworked notice period. For unemployment, the Texas Workforce Commission treats it as a resignation if you gave notice of two weeks or less and the employer let you go early, but if you gave more than two weeks' notice and were let go early without pay, it may be treated as a discharge. Final pay is due by your next regular payday, and unused vacation is owed only if a written policy or agreement says so.

Giving two weeks' notice is a professional courtesy, not a legal duty, and it doesn't protect your job for those two weeks. Before you hand in a resignation letter in Houston, it helps to know what your employer can do next, and what you can still lose.

👉 Notice is a courtesy. Your bonus, PTO and unemployment depend on the details.

1Do You Have to Give Notice?

Most Texas employees work at will, which cuts both ways: your employer can end the job at any time for any lawful reason, and you can leave at any time. Notice may be required only if:

  • You signed an employment contract with a notice period
  • A bonus, retention or equity plan requires notice or continued employment to get paid
  • A company policy ties PTO payout to giving notice (enforceable if written)

Read more about at-will employment vs. right to work in Texas.

2Can Your Employer Make Today Your Last Day?

Yes. Many employers accept a resignation immediately, especially in sales, finance or competitive roles. Under the Texas Payday Law, the employer generally isn't required to pay for the notice period you didn't work, because the duty to pay ends when the separation takes effect.

3Final Pay, PTO and Bonuses

  • Final paycheck: if you resign, it's due by the next regularly scheduled payday. See the Texas Payday Law.
  • Unused vacation or PTO: owed only if a written policy or agreement provides for payout.
  • Bonuses: depend on the plan's terms. Read whether a bonus can be withheld when you quit.
  • Deductions: the employer can deduct from final pay only when authorized by law, a court, or your written permission.

4Unemployment After Resigning

If you quit, Texas generally denies unemployment unless you had good cause connected with the work, such as a major change in pay or duties, unsafe conditions or harassment the employer didn't fix after you reported it. If you're being pushed out, it may matter whether you resign or wait to be fired. Learn more about unemployment if you quit.

Being pressured to resign?

If your employer is offering "resign or be fired," don't sign anything yet. A resignation can affect unemployment, severance and potential claims. Talk to a severance attorney first.

✅How to Resign the Smart Way

  1. Read your contract, bonus plan and PTO policy before you give notice.
  2. Time your notice around bonus payouts and vesting dates if possible.
  3. Give notice in writing and keep a copy with the date.
  4. Save your own records (pay stubs, reviews) before your access is cut off, only what you're allowed to keep.
  5. Ask about references and what will be shared. See what a former employer can say.

?Frequently Asked Questions

Is two weeks' notice required by law in Texas?

No. Texas law does not require employees to give notice before quitting. A notice requirement can come only from a contract or an enforceable written policy.

Can my employer fire me after I give two weeks' notice?

Yes. In an at-will job, the employer can accept your resignation immediately and generally does not have to pay for the unworked notice period, but it cannot end your job early for a discriminatory or retaliatory reason.

Can I get unemployment if my employer lets me go after I give notice?

The Texas Workforce Commission generally treats it as a resignation if you gave two weeks' notice or less. If you gave more than two weeks and were let go early without pay, it may be treated as a discharge.

When do I get my final paycheck if I quit in Texas?

If you resign, your final pay is due by the next regularly scheduled payday after your resignation takes effect.

Do I get paid for unused vacation when I quit in Texas?

Only if your employer's written policy or an agreement provides for vacation payout. Texas law does not otherwise require it.
