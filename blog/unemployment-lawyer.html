Unemployment Lawyer in Texas: Appealing a TWC Denial

Short answer

If the Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) denies your unemployment claim, you have 14 calendar days from the mailing date to appeal to the Appeal Tribunal, which holds a telephone hearing where you and your employer present evidence and witnesses. If you lose, you have another 14 days to appeal to the Commission, and after that you can seek review in civil court within a short window. The hearing is usually your best chance to win, because later reviews generally rely on the hearing record. An unemployment lawyer can prepare your evidence, question the employer's witnesses and spot whether the firing itself was illegal.

A denial letter from TWC isn't the end. Many denials come from the employer's version of events, and the appeal hearing is where your side gets heard. Here's how the process works, the most common reasons for denial, and when it makes sense to bring in a lawyer.

1Common Reasons TWC Denies Benefits

MisconductThe employer says you were fired for misconduct. It generally has to prove it. See unemployment if you were fired.
Quit without good causeYou resigned, or the employer calls it a resignation. See unemployment if you quit.
Severance or pay in lieu of noticeCertain payments delay benefits. See severance and unemployment.
Eligibility issuesNot enough base-period wages, not able and available to work, or work search problems.

2How the Appeal Process Works

  1. Appeal Tribunal: appeal in writing within 14 calendar days of the determination's mailing date, online, by mail, fax or in person. A hearing officer holds a telephone hearing and issues a decision.
  2. Commission appeal: if you lose, appeal in writing within 14 calendar days of the Appeal Tribunal decision's mailing date. The Commission usually reviews the hearing record rather than holding a new hearing.
  3. Motion for rehearing (optional): can be requested within 14 days after the Commission decision is mailed.
  4. Civil court: after completing the TWC appeals, you can seek review in court within a short window after the Commission decision becomes final, generally between 15 and 28 days after it is mailed.
Deadlines run from the mailing date

Not from the day you open the letter. Missing a deadline without good cause can end the appeal.

3How to Win the Telephone Hearing

  • Send your documents in advance, as the hearing notice instructs, to TWC and the employer
  • Focus on the final incident that led to the separation, and any prior warnings
  • Bring firsthand witnesses; secondhand statements carry less weight
  • Cross-examine the employer's witnesses about what they actually saw
  • Be on time and reachable; missing the call can mean losing by default

4When an Unemployment Lawyer Is Worth It

  • The employer is represented or brings several witnesses
  • The employer claims misconduct you dispute, like a policy violation or a drug test
  • You were pressured to resign and TWC treated it as a voluntary quit
  • You think the firing was retaliation or discrimination; the TWC hearing record can matter later
  • You've already lost at the Appeal Tribunal and need to frame a Commission appeal

?Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to appeal an unemployment denial in Texas?

You have 14 calendar days from the mailing date on the TWC determination to file an appeal with the Appeal Tribunal, and another 14 days from the mailing date of that decision to appeal to the Commission.

Do I need a lawyer for a TWC unemployment appeal?

No, but a lawyer can help when the employer claims misconduct, brings witnesses or is represented, or when the firing may have been illegal.

What happens at a TWC appeal hearing?

A hearing officer holds a recorded telephone hearing where the claimant and employer testify, present documents and witnesses, and question each other's witnesses. The officer then issues a written decision.

Should I keep requesting benefits during my appeal?

Yes. Keep requesting payment every two weeks. If you win, TWC pays only for the weeks you requested and were otherwise eligible.

Can I appeal to court if I lose at the Commission?

Yes. After completing the TWC appeal steps, you can seek judicial review in civil court within a short window after the Commission decision becomes final.
