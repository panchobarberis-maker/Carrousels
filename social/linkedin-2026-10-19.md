Can you be fired in Texas for something you posted online?

Often, yes. Texas is an at-will state, and the First Amendment doesn't apply to private employers.

But not every post is fair game. Under the National Labor Relations Act, most private-sector employees, union or not, are protected when they post with or on behalf of coworkers about:

✅ Pay and wages
✅ Safety concerns
✅ Hours and working conditions

A firing can also be illegal when the post is just an excuse, for example when coworkers of a different race, sex or age posted similar things and kept their jobs, or when the firing came right after a discrimination or wage complaint.

If you were fired over a post:
1. Screenshot the post, comments and dates
2. Save the social media policy and any written reason for the firing
3. Act fast: NLRB charges generally must be filed within 6 months
